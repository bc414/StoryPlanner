# reviewing-leads

Enables preparing-a-verification.

| id | mode | instruments | reads | writes | state | description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| review-leads | hitl | | leads results index directions definition calls tally corpus hypothesis-statement | leads question-list | specified | Brian and the session over a study's current leads file, or the current files of two studies of one question: how the exploration ran brought up from the batch's files, any result or the corpus read on his request, a lead checked at the source given a reread line, a fault of the instrument written as a shortcoming, the differences between explorations read as leads about the readers, the questions Brian raises written into the question list, and the sitting closed with his next step for the study |

## Preconditions

A leads file of the study exists; where the question was explored again with the same slices,
each study has one.

## review-leads

The session opens the study's current leads file, its highest-numbered, and first brings up
what orients Brian, read from where each fact lives: the items with results and the writer from the
file's head; the directions the batch ran under, their version, the questions in view their
frontmatter names, what the call was given and what it was never to do; the model and effort
from the definition; the body hash and the harness from the calls file; the itemizer and any
narrowing from the index head; the malformed and missing results from the tally. Where a second
exploration of the question exists, its current leads file is opened beside the first: their
differences are read as leads about the readers, never counted, and what each saw that the other
did not is laid out for Brian. An earlier leads file of the study is read only to review it or to
explain a shortcoming.

Brian reads the leads, and asks for whatever he wants beside them: any individual result, or
the corpus itself. A result is read to trace what a slice reader wrote, never to check a lead,
since a result is as unverified as the lead. When Brian disputes a lead, or asks for one to be
checked, the session follows what the lead points back at: its queries, re-run through the
tool that printed them, by its `run` verb or pasted into its page, to read the table the
session read, the tool saying when the batch has moved on from the count of items with results
the query carries; and its cited items to the results, the item bodies and the corpus at the items'
locators. It reads the corpus where the lead says it was seen, and reports what the source
shows. Each check appends a reread line beneath the lead, dated, with what the source showed,
including whatever part of the lead holds, and saying so where the lead's words are not what
its re-run query's table or its cited results show. The lead itself is not edited, and a lead
whose reread line says the source did not bear it out is not built on.

Where a check shows a standing part of the instrument at fault — the slice chosen at the plan,
the itemizer's cut, directions a faithful reader still went wrong under, a reader not doing what
clear directions asked, the corpus not being what CORPORA.md says — the session writes it under
Shortcomings with its part, and says which step would answer it: a fault of the slice, a new
study through preparing-an-exploration; of the itemizer, a change to the tool through
building-a-tool, cutting a new batch of the study, or a new study where the change alters what
one item is, the corpus read or which items are taken, the review judging which; of the
directions, a new directions version; of the execution, a new study under another model or
effort; of the corpus, a change to the itemizer or a new study, the review judging which. It
starts none of them here. A lead that misstates its own query or cited results is not a fault of
the instrument; its reread line says so. Doubt about `write-leads` itself goes to
revising-the-method.

Work built on leads happens here, with Brian reading the current file: a question raised, an
itemizer or a verification planned, a hypothesis minted. Brian raises questions: from the
Proposed questions, from the leads, from the shortcomings, from the differences, from his own
recall, which enters as a question with its provenance ("Brian's recall, <date>: does the v1
archive show X?"), or goes to minting when he states a prediction. The session writes each into
the question list with what raised it, naming the leads it came from by their tokens, a question
raised from a shortcoming naming the lead that showed it, and a suggested test where one
suggests itself. A lead that shows a different hypothesis is needed is handed to
minting-a-hypothesis in the same session.

The sitting ends with Brian's next step for the study, appended under Next steps in the file
reviewed: to continue the batch, to write a new directions version, to change the itemizer, to
have a new leads file written from the results already present, to start a new study, or to
stop. A step taken because of a shortcoming says so in its why, and a shortcoming he does not act
on stays written whatever step he takes. The why is composed from what he said in the sitting.

## Never

Edits a lead; checks a lead against a result instead of the source; writes a shortcoming the
source or the results did not show; writes a candidate or evidence; rewords a hypothesis; writes
a question Brian did not raise; enters recall as a lead, a finding or evidence; counts
disagreements between lead sets; builds on a lead only an earlier leads file holds; ends a
sitting without its next step, or writes one Brian did not rule.
