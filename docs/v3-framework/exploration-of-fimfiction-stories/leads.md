# exploration-of-fimfiction-stories — leads

## Method

Retroactive. This study is the registry's account of WU1.1, executed on 2026-08-31 under
revision 1 of the method, before studies, reading protocols, the runner or question lists
existed. Scale: pathfinder. One session read the seven meta-analysis reports over the
corpus in one context — 4.1a for the short corpus, 4.2a–e for the long corpus by category
(ensemble; romance and slice-of-life; dark premise; AU; explicit and plot), 4.3 for the
cross-pipeline calibration — and wrote `docs/v3-framework/WU1.1-corpus-synthesis.md`,
which is this study's source and full account; this artifact is its index of leads. No
protocol, no hash, no arms, no runner run; the model and harness were not recorded. The
loci are the reports and the per-story analyses in
`source_material_references/Reading Archive Analyses/`, not the story texts, except where
a later dated review read a text and says so. One arm: no disagreement was measured. Every
count below is a report's own; M4 counts are threshold-dependent across the two analytical
pipelines (report 4.3) and are read qualitatively.

## Questions in view

None from a question list; the lists did not exist. The reading was done with the
revision-1 card's question in view: what the analyzed corpus shows about the mechanism
hierarchy, the DT/FID axis, perception-gap delivery, goals beyond theme, the prose-craft
boundary, length, bonds and revelation, against the fourteen hypotheses the card
targeted. The source is organised by those targets; this index is organised by what was
observed.

## Leads

**Interiority technique and the mechanism ceiling.** Every report pairs sustained FID with
M4 presence and DT-dominant interiority with M4 absence, without dissent, and names the two
reader positions "sympathetic inhabitation" (FID) and "sympathetic observation" (DT). The
P&K analysis (4.2d) uses both techniques and calls the distinction "structurally
load-bearing": Flurry's Direct Thoughts reveal what she conceals, her FID shapes how the
reader processes the narrative. Salvation (4.2c) puts an adversarial inner voice in DT and
the public self-understanding in FID; Not Unless You Mean It puts self-exhortation in DT
and self-deceptive reasoning in FID. Stories with saturated FID (We're Just Feeling Fine,
Controlling Your Desires) reach M4; the analyses describe richer effects in the stories
that vary technique (P&K, Salvation). 4.2b: the three heaviest FID users are mid-range in
length, and the longest stories use FID lightly.

**Ensemble.** All six ensemble stories (4.2a) are DT-dominant and none reaches M4, the
most uniform batch finding; the report proposes that tracking several inner states at
once favours marked interiority. Pinkie Pie is denied focalizer access across several
stories and categories. Omniscient narration appears in one of six; polyamorous
resolutions in two of six.

**Explicit and plot (4.2e).** Ten of eleven stories at DT and ten of eleven absent at M4;
the one M4 story, Controlling Your Desires, is the sole single-focalizer narrative with
FID as its dominant technique. Shame about desire is the defining thematic family in
seven of eleven; sex scenes are read as thematic testing grounds; extended fantasy
sequences are read as unreliable interiority, a pattern specific to this category.

**Dark premise (4.2c).** The two M4 stories, The Haunting of Carousel Boutique and
Salvation, are the two the report finds closest to literary fiction in prose technique.
Chosen family is the dominant theme (four of ten); canon virtues are recontextualised as
psychological traps (Salvation, Dash's New Mom); an institutional-critique pattern recurs.

**AU (4.2d).** M2 is primary or co-primary in seven of eleven; the report describes an
"ambient Latent field", continuous low-level prior revision against canon throughout every
AU story. Leadership and power is the dominant thematic territory (five of eleven);
non-romantic central bonds in five of eleven; genuine counterarguments in eight of
eleven, the corpus's highest rate (P&K, Pax Chrysalia, Best Night Ever). Four AU stories
use a retroactive disclosure about Celestia as a major revelation (Best Night Ever, Moon's
Apprentice, P&K, On a Cross and Arrow).

**Romance and slice-of-life (4.2b).** Six analyses identify dual-POV structural dramatic
irony: the reader holds one focalizer's chapters while reading the other's. Three
stories carry a dual first-person analogue the analyses call "cognitively similar but
formally distinct" from Perception Gap. The wing-flare is a behavioral proxy in ten or
more stories. A conversation-as-resolution pattern recurs, and 4.2b names its circularity:
stories arguing that honest talk resolves conflict demonstrate it by having honest talk
resolve conflict.

**Short corpus (4.1a).** M1 is primary in 75% of stories. Told narration ("she felt X")
is the most common interiority technique. About 24% of stories sit "just below M4": FID
present but too shallow, brief or quickly resolved. Analyses of Walk for Me, Good Grades
Red Flags and Mud Sweat and Tears note powerful effects at M1. The range within M1, "from
simple observation to multi-layered inference from behavioral evidence", is called wider
than the range between some adjacent mechanisms. The vocabulary-compliance section finds no
analysis renaming, paraphrasing or substituting the framework's terms across the 59.

**The M4 gradient.** Four sub-patterns with loci: the flash-gap, opened and closed in one
beat (Finding Your Rhythm's "Yes"); the reader-variable gap (Sweet Nothings, whose
analysis proposes distinguishing reader-variable from reader-guaranteed gaps); the
incipient gap, awareness lagging without sustained FID; narrator intervention, the
contradiction flagged rather than held. Long-corpus stories named as approaching without
reaching: Trial Run, Where Earth Meets Sky, Twilight's List, Third Time's a Charm,
Clocktower Society.

**Gap effects without FID.** Seven techniques with loci. DT-based knowledge asymmetry,
documented in every category (4.1a about six stories; 4.2a four of six; 4.2b six of
fifteen; 4.2e six of eleven), with 4.2b calling the DT/FID boundary "a continuous
technique" the framework "slices through". First-person unreliability in every
first-person story (six short, four romance, one AU): unreliable self-assessment and
first-person enacted irony (Walk for Me), retrospective temporal collapse (Light the Sky on
Fire), epistolary dual positioning (I'll Wait for You); several analyses say the framework
"undersells the reader's experience" there. Dual-POV structural dramatic irony (4.2b,
4.2e). Strategic opacity, one character's interiority withheld to intensify the other's
(Finding Your Rhythm, Sweet Nothings, We're Just Feeling Fine, The Notebook). Narrated
denial (Last Train Home: Rarity describes her blindness after the reader has the
contradicting evidence). The adversarial inner voice (Salvation: "Slut. Whore.
Shameless.", a persona that becomes a literal character in dream sequences). The Mother
voice (Moon's Apprentice: self-criticism externalised as dialogue inside the character's
head, which the analysis places "between Mechanism 3 and Mechanism 4").

**Narrator register and the character's assessment.** Loci the source cited for a
narrator-register rendering of a character's perception: Fixing Up Miss Smartypants's
"semi-FID" or "narrated monologue" (4.2a); Where Earth Meets Sky's focalizer-matched
register (4.2b); P&K's differing FID textures between focalizers and Best Night Ever's
Pinkie sections with "parenthetical interruptions" splitting the narrator's voice (4.2d);
Salvation's differing textures for Rarity and Dash (4.2c). See Corrections for what the
per-story analyses show at those loci. Source-text reading on 2026-09-01 of Where Earth
Meets Sky, Salvation, Best Night Ever and filly-fooling: a continuum from passages where
the character's vocabulary takes over, through a grey zone where neither voice dominates,
to passages whose vocabulary is the narrator's, the clearest being Salvation's "Laconic,
as Rainbow Dash's Word-a-Day calendar might have said"; the narrator-register passages
cluster in one story and around vocabulary-limited characters (Dash).

**Comedy.** Cited as an unmeasured dimension in 85% or more of stories in every
population, almost never as a primary genre. 4.2c: "The warmth that makes the revelations
land is built by the comedy." 4.2d: "The humor does not soften the darkness; it proves the
characters are alive inside it." Pax Chrysalia's comedy "builds the personal relationships
that make the story's betrayals devastating." Per-story review on 2026-09-01 of Best Night
Ever, Fixing Up Miss Smartypants, Magic Tutor, Carrot Top Season and On a Cross and Arrow:
each is structurally doing something else (communication-failure chains, identity
recognition, character transformation) with comedy as the register; only Magic Tutor, A
Horse of a Different Color and a few one-shots are primarily comedies. Structural comedy,
a chain in which each misunderstanding feeds the next (Fixing Up Miss Smartypants), is
distinguished in the reports from one-liner wit.

**Accumulation, setup and relief.** Cumulative emotional investment is cited in half or
more of stories, "substantially more pronounced" in the long corpus (4.2b); 4.1a describes
"individually modest Mechanism 1 instances accumulating to a macro-conclusion" and names it
the framework's "most productive area for potential extension". Prior-belief plants do no
inferential work until their demolition chapters later. Relief at confirmatory resolution,
where the reader already knows and the payoff is affective, is a recurring gap in 4.2a
(four of six ensemble stories). The source names the pathways it sees: presentation to
affect, to accumulation and attachment, to an information plant, to relief, beside
presentation to world inference to theme. In the v2 track definitions, Demonstration
captures each accumulated instance and Reader Opinion the plan, and Reader Opinion's
display question asks about opinion rather than investment.

**The prose-craft phrasing.** Comedy, atmosphere, narrative voice and sensory immersion
are classified as "prose-craft dimensions the framework intentionally does not measure" in
phrasing 4.1a calls "near-formulaic across analyses".

**Length.** Rising with length: M3 instances, obstacle-architecture complexity,
counterargument depth. Thresholds: parallel subplot structures above about 250KB (4.2a);
self-constructed priors that 4.2d calls "qualitatively different" from shorter M2
instances. Not moving with length: M4 (a 1,237KB story in DT throughout reaches it no more
than one at 109KB), M2 density per kilobyte (highest in the shortest stories), FID
prevalence.

**Revelation architecture.** Dramatic irony is the dominant information architecture
across categories, with confirmatory rather than surprising resolution. Architectures
escalate with length: one or two reveals near their setup in short stories,
delayed-detonation reveals at mid length, progressive disclosure, polyphonic convergence
and retroactive recontextualisation in long ones. The source calls retroactive
recontextualisation the corpus's most powerful revelation architecture and notes its
Celestia cluster.

**Counterarguments and world inference.** 76% of the short corpus has no genuine
counterargument (4.1a); ensemble two of six, romance five of fifteen, dark premise five of
ten, AU eight of eleven, explicit about six of eleven. The analyses of Let's Find You a
Date and The AppleDash Project describe tentative or partially defeated resolutions as
more honest endings. Counterargument depth rises with mechanism ceiling and with length.
World-inference chains that serve suspense, prior beliefs or information management
without reaching a thematic claim are common; the ratio varies by category.

**Bonds.** Characterological obstacles are primary in thirteen of fifteen romance stories,
six of ten dark premise, all four bonded ensemble stories, ten of eleven explicit;
communicative obstacles are secondary and read as symptoms; structural obstacles are
rarely primary. Asymmetric interiority access is the norm in dual-focalizer stories;
symmetric access (Parent Trap's omniscient mode, Pax Chrysalia) is the marked case. Every
bond analysis anchors the spark to a specific scene. Bonds are demonstrated through
accumulated gesture, with "I love you" staged as a payoff.

**Embedded texts, dreams, fantasies.** Cited in about 14% of the short corpus (eight
stories) and more in the long corpus: letters doubling the reading position (Letters From
a Secret Admirer, A Certain Type of Chic); dreams as "authored interior experiences that
the character herself does not control" (Perfect on Paper; also Third Time's a Charm,
Inner Strength, Salvation, The Sky is Falling, Flying High Falling Hard, Ribbons and Lace,
Controlling Your Desires); fantasy sequences in explicit and plot stories. The reports
place the phenomenon "between mechanism categories"; 4.1a says it "does not need a
dedicated planning-level track" on the corpus's uses (characterisation, access-mode
shifts).

**Thematic territories.** Five appear in both corpora at comparable frequencies:
vulnerability as prerequisite (about twenty short-corpus stories; ten of fifteen romance;
five of ten dark premise), self-knowledge through experience, love expressed through
behavior, identity as self-authored, connection requiring being seen. Category additions:
leadership and the cost of power (AU), chosen family (dark premise), shame about desire
(explicit).

**Diagnostic profile, as the reports state it.** M2 is called the sharpest tool in every
report; the DT/FID distinction the second; M1 the broadest; M4 the most precise; M3 the
hardest to adjudicate, its boundary between behavioral proxy and "told narration with
behavioral illustration" repeatedly called fuzzy.

**Calibration (4.3).** Cloud and 1M disagree on the M4 threshold in five of eight
evaluable stories (cloud: 29 instances across eight stories; 1M: four). In three of eight,
the 1M analysis reads the whole text but covers only part, invisibly. Cloud analyses
enumerate more instances with less factual reliability; 1M cite fewer with more precision;
below 40KB the difference is negligible.

**Unmapped observations.** The split-self DT dialogue, an inner voice arguing with itself
as a two-character scene (five of eleven explicit, two of six ensemble). The "it's only
practice" fiction, a shared self-deception both characters see through (Professor
Rarity's). Content-rating elision as technique: "The kisses land harder for being the
ceiling of physical expression" (Trial Run). The polyamorous resolution as a completed
structure (two of six ensemble, two of eleven explicit). Structurally significant stasis
(Carrot Top Season's Applejack refuses to change). Perspective breaches clustering at
high-intensity and expository scenes, with no story using a deliberate sustained breach.

## Bins

One arm: no disagreement was measured.

## Proposed questions

- Should M1 be subdivided, or its breadth accepted, given the range 4.1a reports inside it?
- Does the M4 gradient (approached, briefly touched, reader-variable, sustained) need
  representing beyond present or absent?
- Are the narrator-register passages a third cognitive mode, a modifier on the two, or a
  cross-cutting phenomenon, and how many stories show them outside Salvation?
- Does comedy-as-moment placement belong in the planner?
- Is the act of embedding a text or dream a plannable decision beyond the embedded content's
  mechanism, when it serves structural scaffolding or dual-function world infrastructure?
- Could an ensemble story sustain FID across a large cast, or is the DT-ensemble pairing a
  structural necessity?
- Is the Celestia recontextualisation cluster fandom-specific, or a property of a
  deliberately opaque canon character?
- What is the natural ratio of world-inference chains ending in structure to those ending in
  theme, per category and length?
- Which reader-variable gaps register for Brian as a reader, on the loci the analyses name?
