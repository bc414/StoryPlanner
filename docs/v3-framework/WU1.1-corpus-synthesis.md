# WU1.1: Corpus Synthesis

Executed: 2026-08-31
Sources: 7 meta-analysis reports (4.1a, 4.2a-e, 4.3), covering 112 stories

## The DT/FID Axis as Architectural Determinant

The single strongest finding across the entire corpus: the distinction between
Direct Thought (marked interiority) and Free Indirect Discourse (unmarked
narrator-character dissolution) determines the mechanism ceiling of every story.
The correlation is absolute — no report hedges, no analysis dissents.

**The pattern:** Stories with sustained FID can achieve M4. Stories with
DT-dominant interiority cannot, regardless of length, genre, premise, emotional
intensity, or the amount of dramatic irony they sustain. This holds across all
six content categories and both analytical pipelines (cloud and 1M).

**The mechanism:** DT creates what every report calls "sympathetic observation"
— the reader watches a character think from outside a marked window. FID creates
"sympathetic inhabitation" — the narrator's voice dissolves into the character's
consciousness, and the reader absorbs the character's biases as though they were
narration. M4 requires the reader to *inhabit* a self-understanding while holding
contradicting knowledge. DT provides the contradiction (the reader sees the
character is wrong) but not the inhabitation (the reader stays outside). FID
provides both.

**The numbers (qualitative, per the 4.3 calibration caveat):** M4 appears in
roughly 5-8 stories out of 112, depending on how strictly the threshold is drawn.
In the short corpus (4.1a), 2 stories sustain it and 5-6 touch it briefly. In
the long corpus (4.2a-e), sustained M4 appears in The Haunting of Carousel
Boutique and Salvation (dark premise), The Princess and the Kaiser (AU), and
Controlling Your Desires (explicit/plot). Ensemble stories produce zero M4 — the
most uniform batch finding. The 4.3 report documents a systematic calibration
disagreement: the cloud pipeline applies a lower FID threshold for M4 (29
instances across 8 stories) while the 1M pipeline applies a higher one (4
instances). Both positions are defensible readings of the analysis brief's
requirement that M4 "Requires third-person limited with FID." M4 counts are
therefore threshold-dependent and should be treated qualitatively, not compared
across analytical pipelines.

**What the correlation means for the framework:** FID is not a quality marker
— it is a *technique* that enables a specific mechanism. A story that stays in
DT throughout is not inferior; it is operating through a different set of tools
that produce different reader effects. The framework must recognize this
structural relationship without treating it as a quality gradient. This is the
premise of hypothesis 028 (variable focalization) — and the corpus evidence
supports it. The power comes from the *availability* of FID as one mode in a
toolkit, not from its sustained use. P&K's analysis makes this clearest: "The
distinction between DT and FID is structurally load-bearing. Flurry's Direct
Thoughts reveal what she conceals from others; her FID shapes how the reader
processes the entire narrative." Both are doing work. The work is different.


## M1 Dominance: the Structural Baseline

75% of the short corpus operates primarily at M1. The proportion drops somewhat
in the long corpus — ensemble is 5/6, romance/SoL is 8/15, dark premise is 6/10,
explicit/plot is 5/11 — but M1 remains the single most common primary mechanism
in every category except AU, where M2 dominates at 7/11 (see "The AU Exception"
below).

**Why this dominance exists:** Three structural reasons recur across all reports.
First, most stories use told narration ("she felt X") as a primary or secondary
interiority technique, which is M0 by definition and places a ceiling on
sustained mechanism depth. Second, short stories lack the page-time to build and
sustain higher mechanisms. Third, comedy — cited as a significant dimension of
the reading experience in 85%+ of stories — operates through affective registers
the mechanism hierarchy does not capture.

**What M1 dominance does NOT mean:** M1-primary stories are not simpler or less
effective. 4.1a is explicit: several analyses note stories at M1 achieving
powerful effects (Walk for Me, Good Grades Red Flags, Mud Sweat and Tears). The
mechanism hierarchy describes the degree of reader inference required, not the
quality of the reading experience. This is a finding the framework should carry
structurally — the M1 designation should never read as "only M1."

**M1 as a *range*, not a point:** 4.1a identifies M1 as "the broadest category
and the least discriminating: most stories operate here, and the range within M1
(from simple observation to multi-layered inference from behavioral evidence) is
wider than the range between some adjacent mechanisms." This is a diagnostic
finding: the M1 category may be doing too much work. Whether the framework should
subdivide it or accept the breadth is an open question.


## The M4 Gradient

The M4 boundary is not a bright line. 4.1a identifies ~24% of short stories in
the "just below M4" zone: FID is present but too shallow, too brief, or too
quickly resolved to sustain the dual awareness the framework requires. The 4.2
reports confirm this gradient in the long corpus, with stories like Trial Run,
Where Earth Meets Sky, Twilight's List, Third Time's a Charm, and Clocktower
Society all approaching without reaching.

**Four sub-patterns within the gradient:**

1. **The flash-gap:** M4 opened and closed in a single beat. Finding Your
   Rhythm's "Yes" resolves the gap immediately.
2. **Reader-variable M4:** The conditions are present but whether the gap
   registers depends on the individual reader. Sweet Nothings proposes the
   framework should distinguish reader-variable from reader-guaranteed gaps.
3. **The incipient gap:** A character's awareness lags slightly behind the
   reader's, but the narrator does not sustain FID long enough for inhabitation.
4. **Narrator intervention:** The narrator explicitly flags the contradiction
   rather than letting the reader hold it, collapsing the gap into observation.

The framework's binary (present/absent) treatment of M4 does not describe this
territory. The gradient from "not present" through "approached," "briefly
touched," "reader-variable," and "sustained" is a real structural phenomenon the
framework currently compresses into a single boundary.


## Variable Focalization: the Power Is in the Variation

The corpus evidence strongly supports the thesis that the power of perspective
technique comes from *variation* across the focalization spectrum, not from
depth at any one level. The evidence comes from three directions.

**First, the stories with the strongest perspective effects use multiple
techniques.** P&K uses both DT and FID extensively, with the distinction between
them as "structurally load-bearing." Salvation uses DT for the adversarial inner
voice and FID for the public-facing self-understanding, creating a "two-voice
interior monologue." Not Unless You Mean It uses DT for self-exhortation and FID
for sustained self-deceptive reasoning. In each case, the techniques serve
different functions in the same story.

**Second, the "most FID" does not mean "strongest perspective."** Stories with
saturated FID (We're Just Feeling Fine, Controlling Your Desires) achieve M4,
but stories with *varied* technique (P&K, Salvation) achieve richer reader
experiences. The variation creates emphasis — FID hits harder after DT because
the shift itself signals "now you are inside this character's head." Told
narration hits harder after FID because the pull-back signals "now the narrator
is speaking."

**Third, the ensemble finding.** All 6 ensemble stories have zero M4, and all 6
use DT as the dominant technique. The analyses propose a structural explanation:
ensemble stories favor transparent interiority because the reader needs to track
multiple characters simultaneously, and DT's marked boundary makes that tracking
manageable. FID would blur the narrator-character boundary in a story already
blurring boundaries between storylines. The *absence* of focalization variation
in this category is itself evidence that variation is a choice with structural
consequences, not a default.

**The v2 framework centered FID as the primary technique** (the "FID fixation"
chain traced in the hypothesis set). The corpus evidence reframes FID as one
mode in a variable-focalization toolkit. The planner's job should be "design the
focalization trajectory for each scene" — what mix of told narration, DT, FID,
and behavioral proxy — not "ensure FID is present."


## Perception Gap Delivery Beyond FID

The corpus documents at least seven mechanisms that produce M4-adjacent effects
without classical FID-based perception gap:

1. **DT-based knowledge asymmetry.** The most frequently documented technique
   across all reports — appears in every category. When a character states a
   false belief through marked DT, the reader knows the DT is wrong but evaluates
   from outside. The reader performs the same cognitive operation as M4 (holding
   contradicting knowledge about a character's self-understanding) but the
   phenomenological register is different (sympathetic observation vs sympathetic
   inhabitation). The 4.2b report calls this a "continuous technique" with FID-
   based M4, noting the boundary "slices through" what feels like a single
   phenomenon.

2. **First-person unreliability.** All 6 first-person stories in the short
   corpus and all first-person stories in the long corpus note that the
   framework's highest mechanism is structurally unavailable, and identify
   first-person techniques that produce M4-adjacent effects: unreliable self-
   assessment (Walk for Me), retrospective temporal collapse (Light the Sky on
   Fire), first-person enacted irony (Walk for Me), epistolary dual positioning
   (I'll Wait for You). The reader's phenomenological experience — holding what
   the narrator believes against what the reader knows — is similar enough to M4
   that multiple analyses note the framework "undersells the reader's
   experience."

3. **Dual-POV structural dramatic irony.** A new gap identified in the long
   corpus (4.2b, 4.2e). The reader holds knowledge from one focalizer's chapters
   while reading the other's, producing sustained asymmetric awareness across
   multiple chapters. Neither Latent (no single disclosure), nor Perception Gap
   (no FID), nor Development. Six analyses in 4.2b alone identify this.

4. **Strategic opacity.** Deliberate withholding of one character's interiority
   to intensify the reader's experience of the other's. The reader is aware that
   information is being withheld — the technique operates through that awareness.
   Finding Your Rhythm, Sweet Nothings, We're Just Feeling Fine, The Notebook.

5. **Narrated denial.** First-person narrators who tell the reader enough to see
   what they claim not to see. Last Train Home's Rarity describes her own
   blindness while the reader has already accumulated contradicting evidence.
   "A different cognitive operation from Mechanism 4's simultaneous double-
   vision."

6. **The adversarial inner voice.** Salvation's DT operates as a separate
   antagonistic persona — "Slut. Whore. Shameless." — rather than the
   character's own reflection. When externalized in dream sequences, it becomes
   a literal character. Unique in the corpus but analytically significant: a
   "three-layered perspective effect — public mask, private voice, reader's
   independent judgment."

7. **The Mother voice.** Moon's Apprentice externalizes self-criticism as
   dialogue within the character's head. "Between Mechanism 3 and Mechanism 4"
   — the change is not inferred from proxy but identified by an internal voice
   the reader is uncertain whether to trust.

These seven techniques share a common structure: the reader holds knowledge that
contradicts a character's self-understanding, producing dramatic irony through
techniques other than FID. Conversation 21's technique-agnostic gap taxonomy
(ironic/tragic/closing/aligned) appears to be the right framing — the gap is the
designed target, and FID is one of several delivery mechanisms.


## The Narrator-Character Blend

A distinct technique not captured by the current ZF/ND cognitive mode split,
appearing across multiple categories and multiple stories. The narrator's
literary register channels the focalizing character's assessment — the content
is the character's subjective perception, the form is the narrator's vocabulary.
"As if roses were red" — the character notices beauty; the narrator renders that
noticing in language the character would never use.

**Corpus evidence:** 4.2a identifies "semi-FID" or "narrated monologue" in
Fixing Up Miss Smartypants. 4.2b identifies it in Where Earth Meets Sky, where
"each focalizer's vocabulary and cognitive patterns" are "consistently adopted
by the narrator" — Fluttershy's self-minimization, Applejack's values-driven
vocabulary, Dash's competitive framing. 4.2d identifies it in P&K, where the
narrator produces "markedly different FID textures" between focalizers, and in
Best Night Ever's Pinkie sections, where "parenthetical interruptions split the
narrator's voice into two competing registers." 4.2c identifies it in
Salvation's differentiated FID textures between Rarity and Dash.

This is not ZF (the content is subjective, not world-fact). It is not
straightforwardly ND (the narrator's register adds literary texture
independently of the character's cognitive state). It sits between them,
appearing frequently enough across the corpus to be a real technique rather than
an edge case. Whether this requires a third cognitive mode, a modifier on the
existing two, or recognition as a cross-cutting phenomenon is an open question.


## Goals Beyond Theme

The framework gaps sections across all seven reports consistently identify
scene-level effects that never pass through inference and never reach thematic
argument. These are goals peer to theme, with their own delivery pathways.

### Comedy and affect

**The universal gap — and a major structural force.** Cited in 85%+ of stories
across every population, but almost never as a primary genre. Per-story
verification shows even the strongest comedic registers (Fixing Up Miss
Smartypants, On a Cross and Arrow, The Best Night Ever) are structurally doing
something else — communication-failure chains, identity recognition,
character transformation — with comedy as the delivery register. Only a
handful of stories (Magic Tutor, A Horse of a Different Color, possibly a
few short-corpus one-shots) are primarily comedies.

But comedy's near-universal presence as a secondary mode makes it arguably the
most pervasive structural force in the corpus. It does real work: tonal
management (4.2c: "The warmth that makes the revelations land is built by the
comedy; without it, the Latent operations would be melodramatic rather than
moving"), characterization (the reader loves characters partly because they're
funny), reader attachment (4.2d: "The humor does not soften the darkness; it
proves the characters are alive inside it"), and structural setup (Pax
Chrysalia: comedy "builds the personal relationships that make the story's
betrayals devastating").

The framework correctly excludes comedy-as-genre from its measurement scope.
But the exclusion produces a structural blind spot over comedy-as-moment — the
designed placement of comedic beats for tonal management, characterization, or
reader attachment. Whether comedy-as-moment placement belongs in the planner
is an open question.

### Structural setup and prior-belief establishment

These goals serve downstream inference but are themselves non-inferential at the
moment of planting. A prior-belief plant in chapter 3 does no inferential work
for the reader until the demolition in chapter 15. Between planting and
demolition, it serves a structural purpose — managing reader expectations,
creating suspense, establishing the information architecture. The framework
captures the demolition (M2) but has no vocabulary for the planting as a
designed scene-level operation.

### Emotional investment through accumulation

Cited in 50%+ of stories, especially pronounced in longer works. The framework
operates at scene level and does not model cross-scene accumulation:
"individually modest Mechanism 1 instances accumulating to a macro-conclusion"
(4.1a). No single instance of wing-wrapping, food-sharing, or nuzzling carries
great inferential weight, but their accumulation across 10-30 chapters builds an
emotional argument below the scene level. This is the framework's "most
productive area for potential extension" (4.1a) and is "substantially more
pronounced" in the long corpus (4.2b).

### The pathway distinction

These non-thematic goals follow different pathways:
- **P→WI→T** remains the pathway for thematic goals
- **P→affect** for comedy, warmth, atmosphere (no inference stage)
- **P→accumulation→attachment** for emotional investment
- **P→information_plant** for structural setup (stores for later inference)
- **P→relief** for confirmatory resolution (reader already knows; payoff is
  affective, not inferential — 4.2a identifies this as a recurring gap)

Each pathway bypasses the WI→T chain entirely or uses WI for structural purposes
without reaching T.


## The Prose-Craft Boundary

The boundary is remarkably stable across all seven reports. Comedy, atmosphere,
narrative voice, and sensory immersion are consistently classified as "prose-
craft dimensions the framework intentionally does not measure." The phrasing is
"near-formulaic across analyses" (4.1a), suggesting the standing constraint has
been fully internalized.

The corpus evidence suggests the boundary falls on **cross-scene architecture:**

- **Plannable:** Effects that depend on cross-scene architecture — prior-belief
  construction, revelation sequencing, behavioral proxy accumulation, designed
  information asymmetry, obstacle architecture. These require advance design.
- **Prose-craft:** Effects that exist within a single moment of prose — joke
  timing, word choice, atmospheric description, narrative voice register. These
  are chosen at writing time.

The borderland: structural comedy (a comedy-of-errors chain where each
misunderstanding feeds the next — Fixing Up Miss Smartypants) depends on cross-
scene architecture and is therefore plannable. A one-liner wit does not. Comic
*timing* within a scene is prose-craft; comic *architecture* across scenes
(setup/payoff, escalation, the chain) is plannable.


## Length as Structural Enabler

Length effects are consistent across all six substantive reports and divide into
two categories: proportional effects and threshold effects.

### Proportional effects
- **M3 (Development) instances increase with length.** Longer stories have more
  room for behavioral accumulation, producing more sustained character arcs
  tracked across more characters.
- **Obstacle architecture complexity increases with length.** More layered
  obstacle structures with type shifts across phases.
- **Counterargument depth increases with length.** Longer stories afford space
  for the opposing position to be built through backstory rather than compressed
  into a single exchange.

### Threshold effects
- **Parallel subplot structures appear above ~250KB** (4.2a). Below that, a
  single plotline with sequential episodes dominates. This is a structural
  threshold: parallel subplots enable thematic reinforcement through parallelism,
  a gap the framework identifies but cannot measure.
- **Self-constructed priors are length-dependent.** The technique of building a
  prior across multiple chapters before demolishing it requires narrative
  runway that shorter stories cannot afford. 4.2d notes these are "qualitatively
  different" from shorter M2 instances: "the accumulated prior is qualitatively
  different because the reader has invested more of their own interpretive work
  into the belief being demolished."

### What length does NOT affect
- **M4 is determined by technique (FID prevalence), not length.** A 1,237KB
  story using DT throughout is no more likely to achieve M4 than one at 109KB.
- **M2 instance density does NOT increase with length.** Per-kilobyte M2
  density is highest in the shortest stories. M2 instances spread out rather
  than proliferating.
- **FID prevalence does NOT increase with length.** The three heaviest FID
  users in 4.2b are mid-range in length. The longest stories use FID lightly.
  FID is an authorial preference, not a function of available space.


## Category-Specific vs Universal Patterns

### Universal (holds across all six content categories)

- M1 as the most common primary mechanism (except AU)
- DT/FID correlation with M4 achievement: absolute
- Comedy as the universal framework gap: 85%+
- Cumulative effect as under-captured: 50%+
- Behavioral proxy as a significant secondary technique
- The five thematic territories (vulnerability, self-knowledge, love-through-
  action, identity-as-constructed, being-seen) — appear in both corpora at
  comparable frequencies with no territory appearing in one but not the other
- Characterological obstacles as the dominant primary barrier in bonded stories
- Faust-era characterization baseline universal in every category
- M2 as the framework's sharpest diagnostic tool
- The DT/FID distinction as the second sharpest diagnostic

### Category-specific

**AU (4.2d):** The outlier category. M2 dominates (7/11 primary or co-primary)
because AU premises create "ambient Latent fields" — the reader performs
continuous, low-level prior revision throughout every AU story, comparing the AU
version against canonical expectations. Leadership and power is the dominant
thematic territory (5/11), displacing vulnerability. Non-romantic central bonds
appear in 5/11 (the highest proportion). Counterarguments are strongest (8/11
genuine). Genre diversity is highest — AU is a premise choice, not a genre.

**Ensemble (4.2a):** Universal M4 absence (6/6) — the strongest uniform finding
in any batch. Pinkie Pie consistently denied focalizer access across multiple
stories. Omniscient narration rare (1/6) despite ensemble cast. Polyamorous
resolution structures in 2/6. The warm, transparent interiority mode
("sympathetic observation") may be structurally inherent to ensemble comedy.

**Dark premise (4.2c):** Chosen-family theme dominant (4/10). Canon virtues
recontextualized as psychological traps (Salvation, Dash's New Mom). The
adversarial inner voice as a unique technique. Institutional critique pattern.
The two M4 achievers (Haunting, Salvation) are the two that most resemble
literary fiction in prose technique.

**Explicit/plot (4.2e):** Shame-about-desire as the defining thematic family
(7/11). DT dominance is the starkest in the corpus (10/11 at DT, 10/11 absent
at M4). Sex scenes as thematic testing ground rather than decoration. Fantasy
sequences as unreliable interiority — a gap specific to this category. The one
M4 story (Controlling Your Desires) is the sole single-focalizer narrative with
FID as its dominant technique; the correlation is absolute within the batch.

**Romance/SoL (4.2b):** Dual-POV structural dramatic irony as a new gap (6
analyses). Wing-flare as universal behavioral proxy (10+ stories). The
structural analogue to M4 through dual first-person POV (3 stories). The
conversation-as-resolution pattern as a potential narrative shortcut.

**Short corpus (4.1a):** 75% M1 dominance. Told narration as the most common
interiority technique. M4 gradient documented most precisely (24% of stories in
the "just below" zone). The framework's vocabulary functioning as a shared
analytical language validated by the vocabulary compliance section.


## Revelation Architecture and WI-Terminal Patterns

### Revelation architecture across the corpus

**Dramatic irony (reader ahead of characters)** is the dominant information
architecture in the majority of stories across all categories. The reader holds
superior knowledge for sustained periods; resolution is confirmatory rather than
surprising — the payoff is affective (relief, warmth, catharsis) rather than
inferential.

The specific architectures escalate with length:
- **Short stories:** One or two major reveals within a chapter of their setup
- **Mid-length:** Delayed-detonation reveals with multi-chapter distances
- **Long stories:** Progressive disclosure spanning hundreds of pages,
  polyphonic convergence, retroactive recontextualization of the entire story

An opinionated finding: **retroactive recontextualization is the corpus's most
powerful revelation architecture.** Best Night Ever's Celestia reveal, Moon's
Apprentice's Celestia's Plan B, P&K's Celestia abdication, On a Cross and
Arrow's Celestia-Solaris mirror — the strongest retroactive moments share a
pattern: they involve Celestia, whose canonical inscrutability makes her a
natural vessel for hidden knowledge. This may be fandom-specific, but the
technique (a disclosure that forces the reader to reinterpret everything they've
already read) is the most powerful M2 operation in the corpus.

### WI-terminal ratio

The counterargument deficit provides evidence for the WI-terminal hypothesis.
76% of short-corpus stories have no genuine counterargument (4.1a). This means
most WI chains serve structural purposes — building suspense, establishing
prior beliefs, managing information — without reaching thematic argument. WI is
doing work that never terminates at T.

This ratio varies by category: AU stories have the highest counterargument rate
(8/11 genuine in 4.2d), suggesting more T-terminal chains. Romance and ensemble
stories have the lowest, suggesting the genre contracts promise resolution
without demanding thematic contest.

The v2 rule from conversation 47 ("every link must have T") appears overfit.
The corpus shows WI-terminal chains are not deficient — they serve legitimate
structural purposes. The natural ratio of WI-terminal to T-terminal varies by
category and genre, and neither ratio is inherently better.


## Bond Architecture Patterns

**Characterological obstacles dominate every category.** In romance/SoL, 13/15
stories have characterological primary obstacles. In dark premise, 6/10. In
ensemble, all 4 bonded stories. In explicit/plot, 10/11. Communicative
obstacles are consistently secondary — symptoms of the characterological
barrier, not independent problems. Structural obstacles are rare as primary
barriers. The dominant pattern is: the character must change before the
relationship is possible.

**Asymmetric interiority access is the norm, not the exception.** In most
dual-focalizer stories, one partner receives deeper interiority access. This
asymmetry is structurally productive — it creates dramatic tension about the
less-accessed partner's feelings. Symmetric access (Parent Trap's omniscient
mode, Pax Chrysalia's dual access) is the marked case, identified as a
deliberate architectural choice.

**The Spark is specific.** Every bond analysis across all reports traces the
romantic spark to a specific past moment or quality, not a generalized
attraction. The specificity is consistent — each spark is anchored to a
particular scene.

**Demonstration over declaration.** All reports confirm that bonds are
demonstrated through behavioral evidence and accumulated gesture rather than
verbal declaration. Stories that include "I love you" moments stage them as
payoffs earned by chapters of behavioral demonstration.


## Embedded Texts, Dreams, and the Double Interpretive Layer

Present across all populations, cited in ~14% of the short corpus and more
prominently in the long corpus:

- **Embedded texts** (letters, diaries, notebooks) create a doubled reading
  position: the reader evaluates the document AND reads the character reading
  the document (Letters From a Secret Admirer, A Certain Type of Chic)
- **Dream sequences** as psychological evidence — not behavioral proxy in the
  standard sense, not FID, not told narration. They are "authored interior
  experiences that the character herself does not control or understand"
  (Perfect on Paper). Present in dark premise (Third Time's a Charm, Inner
  Strength, Salvation), romance/SoL (Perfect on Paper, The Sky is Falling,
  Flying High Falling Hard), and explicit/plot (Ribbons and Lace, Controlling
  Your Desires)
- **Fantasy sequences** as unreliable interiority — specific to the explicit/
  plot category, where extended sexual or romantic fantasies reveal
  psychological deterioration

These share a structure: content operates through one interpretive frame while
the relationship between the embedded content and the embedding narrative
operates through another. The mechanism is not the embedded content's own
presentational mode — it is the *act of embedding*, creating a double
interpretive layer. The framework currently classifies embedded content by its
internal mechanism (a letter is M1 within its own frame) but the reader's
experience includes a second operation (why did the author include this? what
does it reveal about the character reading/dreaming it?).

This is a real structural phenomenon whose impact is an open question. The
reports consistently note it "sits between mechanism categories" without
proposing a solution, and 4.1a notes it "does not need a dedicated planning-
level track" — but that assessment is based on the corpus's use of embedded
texts (characterization, access-mode shifts), which may be narrower than what
a multi-story project does with them. If embedded texts serve as structural
scaffolding (subplot transitions, information architecture across focalizers)
or as dual-function world infrastructure (simultaneously psychological
evidence and plot-functional communication), the planning-level significance
is higher than the corpus alone suggests. Whether the framework should
represent the *act of embedding* as a plannable design decision — beyond
classifying the embedded content's internal mechanism — is an open question
for WU1.3, WU1.4, and WU1.9.


## The Five Thematic Territories

The same five thematic territories appear across both corpora at comparable
frequencies, with no territory appearing in one but not the other:

1. **Vulnerability as prerequisite** (~20 stories in the short corpus, 10/15 in
   romance/SoL, 5/10 in dark premise). The corpus's dominant thematic territory.
2. **Self-knowledge through experience** (~12 short corpus, 9/15 romance/SoL).
   Often Twilight-focused: analytical nature must yield to embodied experience.
3. **Love expressed through behavior** (~10 short corpus, 8/15 romance/SoL).
4. **Identity as self-authored** (~8 short corpus, 7/15 romance/SoL, 3/11 AU).
5. **Authentic connection requiring being seen** (~6 short corpus, 6/15
   romance/SoL).

**Category-specific additions:**
- AU: Leadership and the cost of power (5/11)
- Dark premise: Chosen family constituted by choice (4/10)
- Explicit/plot: Shame about desire causing more harm than the desire itself
  (7/11)

The stability of the five core territories is itself a finding: these
propositions are properties of the genre (MLP romance fanfiction) rather than
of individual stories. The framework should expect them rather than discovering
them anew in each analysis.


## Counterargument Architecture

The counterargument deficit is a genre phenomenon, not a deficiency of
individual stories. It correlates with genre contract: romantic comedies and
slice-of-life stories rarely present genuine resistance to their theses because
the genre promises resolution.

**The distribution:** Short corpus 12% genuine, ensemble 2/6, romance/SoL 5/15,
dark premise 5/10, AU 8/11, explicit/plot ~6/11.

AU stories have the strongest counterarguments — P&K, Pax Chrysalia, and Best
Night Ever present genuinely defensible opposing positions that are never
dismissed. This may reflect the AU category's thematic preoccupation with power
and leadership, which naturally produces genuine opposition.

**An opinionated finding:** Stories with genuine counterarguments are generally
stronger for it. The analyses note that Let's Find You a Date's tentative
resolution and The AppleDash Project's partially defeated counterargument
produce more honest endings than stories where all positions align behind the
thesis. Counterargument depth correlates with mechanism ceiling and with length
— stories at M2+ with strong counterarguments tend to be longer, suggesting
both require narrative runway.


## Methodological Foundation

### Framework fidelity

4.1a's vocabulary compliance section validates that the v4 Brief functions as a
shared analytical language across all 59 short-corpus analyses. "No analysis
renames, paraphrases, or substitutes the framework's vocabulary." "No partial
adoptions, no terminology drift, no standing-constraint violations." This
validates the cross-report comparison that the synthesis performs — the
vocabulary means the same thing in every analysis.

### Calibration caveats (from 4.3)

Two systematic differences between the cloud and 1M pipelines:

1. **The M4 threshold disagreement.** Cloud identifies M4 where 1M denies it,
   in 5 of 8 evaluable stories. The cloud applies a lower threshold for what
   constitutes "sufficient FID." The disagreement is analytical, not coverage-
   driven — it reflects a calibration difference between independent sessions.
   **Consequence:** M4 counts are threshold-dependent. Cross-pipeline M4
   comparison is confounded. This synthesis treats M4 qualitatively (present/
   absent/approached) throughout.

2. **The 1M analysis-truncation failure mode.** In 3 of 8 evaluable stories,
   the 1M version reads the full text but produces analysis covering only part.
   This is invisible — the analysis reads as complete. **Consequence:** 1M-only
   analyses (Subsets B and C in the long corpus) may undercount mechanisms.

3. **The compaction trade-off.** Cloud analyses enumerate more instances but are
   less factually reliable on details. 1M analyses cite fewer instances but are
   more precise. For the 57 short-corpus analyses (all below 40KB), compaction
   effects are negligible. For the long corpus, the full corpus can be treated
   as a single dataset with the M4 caveat and the truncation risk.


## Corpus-Level Diagnostic Profile

An opinionated assessment of the framework's diagnostic power, drawn from the
consistency of findings across all seven reports:

**Sharpest tool: M2 (Latent).** Every report independently identifies M2 as the
most precisely captured mechanism. The prior-disclosure-revision structure maps
directly onto the reader's cognitive process, the requirement to name the
prior's source produces specific findings, and it works across every genre from
light comedy to military epic. This is the framework's strongest contribution.

**Second sharpest: the DT/FID distinction.** Load-bearing wherever it operates.
Predicts mechanism ceiling, produces falsifiable claims about individual
passages, and holds across every category and length.

**Broadest: M1 (Enacted).** The range within M1 is wider than the range between
some adjacent mechanisms. Most stories operate here. The framework correctly
identifies M1 work but the category is the least discriminating.

**Most precise: M4 (Perception Gap).** Strict requirements, rare instances. Its
precision makes it useful as a diagnostic when present and as a meaningful
absence when not. The framework provides the vocabulary to describe what a
story's technique *prevents* it from doing.

**Hardest to adjudicate: M3 (Development).** The boundary between behavioral
proxy (reader infers change) and "told narration with behavioral illustration"
(narrator states change and shows evidence) is frequently noted as fuzzy. This
is a vocabulary gap.


## Emergent Patterns

### The ensemble-DT-comedy relationship

Ensemble stories uniformly produce zero M4 with DT-dominant interiority and
heavy comedy. 4.2a proposes this is structural: "ensemble comedies favor
transparent interiority because the reader needs to track multiple characters'
inner states simultaneously." Whether this is a genre convention or a structural
necessity — whether an ensemble story *could* sustain FID across a large cast
— is untested.

### The confession-as-resolution pattern

Multiple reports (4.2b, 4.2c) note a pattern where characters resolve emotional
problems through a single extended honest conversation. Several analyses
identify this as a potential shortcut that "bypasses more protracted emotional
processing." The pattern is specific to romance's commitment to communication as
theme — the stories argue that honest talk resolves conflict, and demonstrate
this by having honest talk resolve conflict — and 4.2b calls the circularity
explicitly.

### The Celestia-as-recontextualization-vessel pattern

Four AU stories use retroactive disclosure about Celestia as a major revelation
architecture element. Her canonical inscrutability makes her a natural site for
hidden knowledge. This is fandom-specific but structurally interesting: a
character whose canon characterization is deliberately opaque becomes a vessel
for the strongest M2 operations in the corpus.

### Pinkie Pie as consistent non-focalizer

Across multiple stories in multiple categories, Pinkie is present but denied
narrative interiority, making her operate at M3 for the reader regardless of the
story's primary mechanism. This appears to reflect a shared authorial instinct:
Pinkie's unpredictability is preserved by keeping the reader outside her
consciousness.


## Unmapped Observations

Findings that don't map cleanly to the 14 target hypotheses:

1. **The split-self DT dialogue** — a character's inner voice divides into
   competing voices that argue with each other. Present in 5/11 explicit/plot
   stories, 2/6 ensemble stories, and scattered elsewhere. Not a simple DT
   instance — it dramatizes internal conflict as a two-character scene. The
   framework has no category for this.

2. **The "it's only practice" fiction** — characters maintaining a shared self-
   deception that enables intimacy while protecting against vulnerability
   (Professor Rarity's). The reader sees through it immediately; the technique
   is the characters' mutual maintenance of a lie they both see through.

3. **Content-rating elision as technique** — what a story refuses to show does
   real work. Stories gain power from restraint. "The kisses land harder for
   being the ceiling of physical expression" (Trial Run). The framework
   captures what is present but has no vocabulary for the productive absence.

4. **The polyamorous resolution** — appears in ensemble (2/6) and explicit/plot
   (2/11) categories. Triads treated as completed structures, not love triangles
   to be resolved. Requires the character to overcome the belief that love is a
   zero-sum resource.

5. **Anti-development / structurally significant stasis** — Carrot Top Season's
   Applejack refuses to change despite mounting evidence. The framework captures
   behavioral proxies of near-breakdown but has no category for "development
   that does not occur as a structurally significant absence."

6. **The AU ambient Latent field** — persistent background comparison between
   canon and AU. Not a single M2 instance but a continuous reading mode that
   enriches every moment. Multiple reports identify it but the framework has no
   term for it.

7. **Perspective breaches clustering at specific scene types** — high emotional
   intensity and expository needs. No story in the corpus uses deliberate
   sustained perspective breach as a technique. This absence is itself a finding.
