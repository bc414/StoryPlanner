### own-fiction/narrator-register-or-focalizer-voice

- date: 2026-09-13
- raised by: the migration review of hypothesis 030, when its chain turned out to hold a
  question rather than a claim about the planner: "It seems like this chain is more of
  pointing towards a question for own-corpus". The chain began at a line of the naive TLTT
  chapter 1 that an assistant had quoted to him: "this is really interesting with 'as if
  roses were red'. Is this AJ's FID or an omniscient narrator's injected wit? I wrote this
  instinctively back then and it made me feel something rereading it. 'while the Tall River
  ran red' also seems like some sort of distinct narrator, but not necessarily AJ's FID. This
  is what I mean by third person limited but without a plan." An assistant's answer named it
  a narrator-character blend and called it a technique of its own; a later assistant reading
  of corpus passages called it FID with varying narrator presence, which he took up, adding:
  "I'm particularly interested in testing this against my written work." Reading his own
  passages afterwards: "I thought the 'pink curtain' motif for Fluttershy in Green Is Your
  Color was a narrator voice/narrator blend. Fluttershy wouldn't call her own mane a pink
  curtain?"; of a Shane passage, "This is Shane's voice remarking sardonically on his
  parents' attitudes, not a narrator voice."; of Wish's narrator asides, "probably unusual
  and not something I'd do again".
- question: In Brian's own fiction, where narration renders what a focalizing character
  perceives or judges, is it carried in the character's own vocabulary or in a narrator's
  register the character would not use?
- suggested test: "Look at the actual source text of the examples to make a determination";
  "read the paragraph" rather than the passage. For each such line, note whether its
  wording is one the character uses elsewhere in the same text.
