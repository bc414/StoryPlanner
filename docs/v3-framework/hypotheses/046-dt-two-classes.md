## Hypothesis

Heavy DT in Brian's fiction is not one technique but two: DT that produces
sympathetic observation or a DT-based perception gap (the reader holds the
marked thought against contradicting evidence), and DT that is told
interiority in italics — stating a feeling or realization the surrounding
text could have led the reader to infer. The second class is the
interiority-layer analogue of fabula-through-dialogue: statement standing in
for inference.

## Origin

- date: 2026-09-01
- reasoning: Raised during the WU1.3 post-review of NTL, when Brian read two
  passages a classification pass had flagged: line 6683, where the guilt of
  being thanked by a man whose village Windie's own side had wronged is
  carried by told narration ("Windie felt like a Stone Edge was slicing
  through his chest") and then a marked thought ("Is Luneland worth
  preserving?"); and line 8649, where Lily's already-established childlike
  register ("snuggle," "pitter-patter") is displaced by the narrator naming
  her state directly ("an itch that gnawed at her, forcing her to imagine
  what it was like to live under pressure"). Brian's own reading of both:
  neither is FID, and the marked thought at 6683 is on the page when it
  would serve better left to be inferred; the itch at 8649 is the same
  pattern of told interiority stepping in where the register already running
  could have carried the feeling. From there his broader assertion: his
  heavy use of DT is not a uniform conclusion. Some of it does the work of a
  reader-held gap, where a marked thought is weighed against contradicting
  or fuller knowledge; some of it is a crutch of told interiority in
  italics, stating what other mechanisms would have led the reader to infer
  — the same failure shape, one layer down, as a fabula stated in dialogue
  instead of left to be inferred. The motivation for treating this as its
  own claim rather than a restatement of the existing DT finding: the WU1.3
  synthesis had reported DT dominance as a uniform count, and Brian's
  reading of these two passages is that the count may be uniform while the
  function behind it is not.
