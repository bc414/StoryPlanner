## Hypothesis

Without a system prompt, Claude often makes analytical claims without checking them against
the evidence unless Brian asks it to.

## Origin

- date: 2026-09-12
- reasoning: Brian's observation comes from his own v2-era use of Claude. In his Keep notes:
  "Recall that I liked Sonnet 4.6 for narrative analysis (still my preferred) because it worked
  better without a system prompt and its voice is helpful and accommodating. But I still had to
  ask for grounding." He had dropped system prompts for v2, "In the v2 Claude era, I did NOT use
  ANY system prompts", after Claude's early work satisfied him, on what he called "my old working
  theory was Claude=good, Gemini=bad." On 2026-08-29, about to write a skill for hypothesis
  iteration, he revised it: "some examples in the apples to apples comparison suggests that
  Claude was more rigorous than Gemini but not more 'scientifically grounded' and I had always
  had to prompt for evidence grounding. Maybe Claude less so than Gemini." His assertion: "Claude
  is better at 'linting' by default since constitutional AI RLHF instructs it to not make false
  claims and be rigorous, but the evidence of past convos would show that it doesn't always do
  the verification, and a system prompt would make it more ironclad." The motivation is how v3's
  instructions get designed: "System prompt and skills should not be a top down approach like
  before, just iterating on first principles" and "It should be based on evidence of what worked
  it didn't work in conversation history". His account of the shift: "The paradigm shift is that
  I thought before that Claude was good because out of the box behavior was good. I was only
  using first principles to ask questions. Ai studio system prompting felt futile but it wasn't a
  dead end. Now I'm using 9 months of historical prompting to inform my design of the
  instructions." Claude drafted the statement's wording on 2026-09-12 from these passages, and
  Brian adopted it. It states only the gap without a system prompt, not whether a system prompt
  closes it.
