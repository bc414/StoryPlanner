## Hypothesis

Different stories in the multi-story project need different variable-focalization
mixes — Chrysalis's Greek tragedy is not TLTT's ensemble is not Kitty's
bildungsroman — supported by one universal track architecture with variable usage
density per story rather than per-story track definitions.

## Record

- created | 2026-08-31T20:00: The multi-story project spans paradigms that make
  fundamentally different demands on perspective technique. Chrysalis's story is
  a Greek tragedy with a single dominant focalizer whose interiority the reader
  accesses deeply and whose downfall the reader watches with full dramatic irony
  — heavy perception-gap work, deep sustained focalization, the reader knowing
  more than Chrysalis does. TLTT is an ensemble with multiple focalizers whose
  partial perspectives the reader assembles into a composite understanding — the
  reader's knowledge comes from accumulating partial views, and prior-belief
  management across focalizer transitions is the central design problem. Kitty's
  bildungsroman tracks one character's growth through a world that changes around
  her — bond and development tracking, the reader growing alongside the
  character. These are not different stories that happen to use the same
  framework; they are different paradigms that require different emphasis within
  the framework. The hypothesis is that a single track architecture serves all of
  them — the same set of tracks, the same mechanism vocabulary, the same
  inference-stage model — with different usage profiles per story. Chrysalis's
  scenes populate perception-gap tracks densely and development tracks lightly;
  TLTT's scenes populate prior-belief and transition tracks densely; Kitty's
  scenes populate bond and growth tracks densely. The architecture is universal;
  the density is per-story. This predicts that no track should be story-specific,
  and that apparent story-specificity signals a track that is too narrow.
