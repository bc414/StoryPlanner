## Hypothesis

Different stories in the multi-story project need different focalization-technique
mixes rather than one shared profile: Chrysalis's story, a Greek tragedy whose reader
already knows her downfall, calls for markedly heavier use of character-reader
perception-gap technique than TLTT's ensemble of focalizers or Kitty of Westkeep's
bildungsroman do. The same track architecture serves every story in the project, with
how densely each track is used varying by story rather than any story needing a track
of its own.

## Origin

- date: 2026-08-31
- reasoning: Raised in a 2026-08-28/29 conversation about the corpus-analysis
  synthesis pipeline, as Brian registered that the multi-story project had grown past
  The Lioness of Tall Tale itself to the parallel and prequel stories developing
  alongside it, and that the project was not one-size-fits-all: different stories
  call for different amounts of the same techniques. His assertion rested on three
  stories he characterized as different paradigms: TLTT, which he described as an
  ensemble — Applejack and Twilight as its primary focalizers, the rest of the mane
  six also focalizing, and the wider multi-story project even more of an ensemble
  once prequel-sequels bring in their own focalizers; Chrysalis's prequel-sequel,
  which he called a Greek tragedy — the reader already knows she loses the Great War,
  and the spectacle is inhabiting who she was on the way there — which he expected
  would lean disproportionately on character-reader perception-gap technique; and
  Minette's The Kitty of Westkeep, which he described as able to stand on its own as
  an OC bildungsroman with explicit content fundamental to its plot, built on the
  Aquileian lore he had developed. Asked to ground the claim against the working plan
  rather than argue it in the abstract, he pointed out that a subject-wide
  perception-gap track already exists and is already in use on Chrysalis's story —
  his own evidence that her story's emphasis needs heavier use of an existing track,
  not a track of its own.
