## Hypothesis

DT-based knowledge asymmetry (deep third revealing what a character knows that
the reader doesn't, or vice versa) is a real technique adjacent to M4/dramatic
irony, distinct enough from FID-delivered irony to warrant separate recognition
in the framework.

## Record

- created | 2026-08-31T20:00: From the original 8 hypotheses derived from 42
  stories. Deep third (close third-person narration restricted to one
  character's perspective) can create knowledge asymmetry in two directions:
  the character knows something the reader doesn't (because the narration
  touches the knowledge without explaining it — the character takes it for
  granted), or the reader knows something the character doesn't (because the
  reader has accumulated information from other scenes or other characters'
  DT sections that this character lacks). Both produce reader inference — the
  reader either works to figure out what the character knows, or watches the
  character act in ignorance of what the reader knows. This is adjacent to
  M4 (dramatic irony) but operates through a different mechanism: M4 in the
  classical sense requires the reader to recognize the irony (the character
  says X, the reader knows not-X). DT knowledge asymmetry can be subtler —
  the reader may not realize they know more or less than the character until
  the asymmetry produces consequences. The distinction matters for the
  framework because it predicts different planning needs: classical M4 is
  designed at the scene level (set up the ironic gap, deliver the ironic
  moment), while DT knowledge asymmetry may be an emergent property of the
  focalization plan (which characters get DT sections, in what order, with
  what information restrictions). If the distinction holds, the planner needs
  vocabulary for both.
- evidence | 2026-08-31T23:30 | (WU1.1) [supporting]: DT-based knowledge
  asymmetry is the most frequently documented unnamed technique across the
  entire corpus — it appears in every category. 4.1a identifies it in ~6
  stories, 4.2a documents it in 4/6 ensemble stories, 4.2b in 6/15 romance/SoL
  stories, 4.2e in 6/11 explicit/plot stories. Every report uses the same
  diagnostic language: "sympathetic observation rather than sympathetic
  inhabitation." The 4.2b report calls the DT/FID boundary "a continuous
  technique" that the framework "slices through," confirming the technique is
  real and distinct from FID-delivered M4.
