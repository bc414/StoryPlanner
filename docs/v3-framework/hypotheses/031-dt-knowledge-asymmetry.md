## Hypothesis

DT-based knowledge asymmetry — deep third narration in which a character's marked
thought is demonstrably wrong while the reader recognizes the wrongness from
outside the character's perspective, rather than inhabiting it through FID — is a
real, distinct technique. It sits adjacent to M4/dramatic irony but is separable
from the FID-delivered irony of the Perception Gap mechanism, producing
sympathetic observation rather than sympathetic inhabitation.

## Origin

- date: 2026-08-31
- reasoning: Raised from the original eight corpus-level findings surfaced by the
  42-story meta-analysis (2026-08-17), which named DT-based knowledge asymmetry
  as a real technique adjacent to M4. In deliberation the next day, while working
  through the italics epiphany that had implicitly ranked direct thought (DT)
  below free indirect discourse (FID), the session worked out what made DT's
  version of the reader-character knowledge gap different from FID's: FID's
  Perception Gap mechanism makes the reader inhabit a character's wrong
  self-understanding from inside the narration, while DT lets the reader hear the
  same wrongness as a quoted thought and judge it from outside — sympathetic
  observation rather than sympathetic inhabitation. Brian then asked directly
  whether the existing Perception Gap track should be generalized to cover this:
  "So should perception gap be generalized to knowledge asymmetry?" The
  motivation was to determine whether DT's route to the gap needed its own
  recognition or could be folded into the Perception Gap mechanism already in
  the planner.
