## Hypothesis

Comedy, atmosphere, and narrative voice are prose-craft — chosen at writing
time, not planned in the planner — rather than framework-level categories.

## Origin

- date: 2026-08-31
- reasoning: Raised in the 2026-08-17 meta-analysis of 51 pipeline outputs
  across 42 stories, where roughly fifteen analyses flagged comedy as a blind
  spot the mechanism × inference-stage matrix could not capture — the matrix
  records what a reader concludes, not what they feel or find funny. Brian,
  replying to the meta-analysis on 2026-08-18 (session `e16846b2`, seq 77),
  drew the boundary himself: "the framework is NOT intended to be EVERYTHING
  that comes to writing... Only the 'hard' parts (more technical) which I
  didn't have the vocab for before." Comedy he held with full confidence:
  "Comedy comes from writing the prose. I know how to do that in my own way,
  and even if I didn't it ought not to be something defined analytically in a
  sqlite database of the fabula and syuzhet. That is prose craft, and AI
  assistance should not go anywhere near, unlike the off-the-page fabula
  (world building) and syuzhet (narrative architecture)." Atmosphere and
  narrative voice he raised more tentatively than comedy, as open questions
  rather than as settled alongside it: "I believe the same must be allied to
  the Atmospheric/affective immersion aspect that was called out? This seems
  like a critical question to address," and, of narrative voice, "Needs to be
  analyzed whether they belong in the fabula/syuzhet database... or if they
  should be quarentined to the 'writing techniques' subjects as
  reference/guidance/notes to self but not instrumentalized across the story
  planner." The motivation is the framework's own declared scope — it is
  constrained to the fabula and syuzhet, not to everything that goes into
  writing — and these three are the candidates for what sits outside it.
  Revisited in session `f32f0c91` on 2026-09-12, Brian: "I don't know how I
  lean on atmosphere or narrative voice."
