## Hypothesis

Three distinct concerns — goal (what the scene is FOR), mechanism (what the
reader does), and prose technique (how it's written) — should be recognized
separately by the planner, with the reader-experience-moment as the unit of
designed reader operation at scene-level intersections.

## Record

- created | 2026-08-31T20:00: The three concerns emerged from the synthesis
  conversation's analysis of where v2's vocabulary ran thin. Goal is the
  designer's intent for a moment: thematic evidence, emotional investment,
  prior-belief establishment, structural setup. Mechanism is the cognitive
  operation the reader performs: inference from subtext, recognition of pattern,
  gap detection between character and reader knowledge. Prose technique is the
  rendering: FID, direct narration, behavioral description, dialogue subtext.
  The key insight is that these are NOT a pipeline (goal → mechanism →
  technique) but three simultaneous properties of a single designed moment.
  The reader-experience-moment is the unit — an instance of designed reader
  operation at a scene-level intersection (a plot point × subject link). A
  moment simultaneously HAS a goal, operates through a mechanism, and is
  rendered via a technique. The codebase's Note is the hierarchical
  representation (one Note, one Track, one TrackType); the moment is the
  potentially dimensional reality. Whether the planner's representation should
  evolve toward the moment or remain Note-based with the moment as a conceptual
  overlay is itself an open question — but the three concerns must be
  recognizable regardless of representation. Prose techniques are never planned
  (Brian's hypothesis that he can choose them instinctively at writing time);
  goals and mechanisms belong in the planner. Split from a larger hypothesis
  that also addressed how the three concerns relate structurally — the
  structural question is in hypothesis 024.
- evidence | 2026-08-31T23:30 | (WU1.1) [supporting]: The corpus reports
  consistently separate three concerns in their section structure: mechanism
  distribution (what the reader does — M1-M4), perspective technique (how it's
  rendered — FID, DT, told narration, behavioral proxy), and framework gaps /
  cross-cutting findings (what goals the scene serves — theme, comedy, emotional
  investment, structural setup). These three vary independently across stories:
  the same mechanism serves different goals, the same technique operates at
  different mechanism levels, and the same goal is achieved through different
  techniques. Non-thematic goals (comedy, emotional investment, structural
  setup) follow pathways that bypass inference entirely, confirming they are
  peer concerns, not sub-concerns of theme.
