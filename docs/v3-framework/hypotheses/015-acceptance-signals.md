## Hypothesis

When Brian copy-pastes AI output into the .storyplan, that act is a positive
signal about the output's quality — analogous to an RLHF reward — and the
prompting context that produced it is therefore evidence for how instructions
should be designed.

## Origin

- date: 2026-08-31
- reasoning: Raised in a Google Keep note, written before 2026-08-30, in which
  Brian was arguing that system prompts and skills should be built from
  evidence of what had worked across his conversation history rather than
  iterated from first principles. In that context he noticed his own
  behavior: copy-pasting AI output directly into the .storyplan. His words:
  "The copy pasted data is good for this! It's a signal that I liked the
  output. So whatever prompt is probably good." His assertion is that the
  copy-paste act itself is a positive quality signal, comparable to an RLHF
  reward, and that the prompting context behind a copied passage is therefore
  evidence for how instructions ought to be designed. He went on to ask, in
  the same note, what other signals beyond copy-paste might mark acceptance
  and whether industrial RLHF, fine-tuning or post-training technique could be
  borrowed to mine them — a question the statement does not carry and leaves
  to evidence.
