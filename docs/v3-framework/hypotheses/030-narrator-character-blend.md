## Hypothesis

The narrator-character blend ("as if roses were red" — the narrator's literary
register channeling the focalizer's assessment) is a distinct technique not
captured by the current ZF/ND cognitive mode split.

## Origin

- date: 2026-08-31
- reasoning: Raised while Brian was rereading his own naive TLTT chapters and
  noticing a line he had written instinctively: "'Because Tall Tale is my
  home,' Comet Shine said, as if roses were red." He flagged a second instance
  from the same material, "while the Tall River ran red." His own question
  about the first: "Is this AJ's FID or an omniscient narrator's injected
  wit?" — and about the second, that it "also seems like some sort of
  distinct narrator, but not necessarily AJ's FID." He named this his
  experience of "third person limited but without a plan": he could feel
  something was happening in the prose but had no vocabulary for it. The
  corpus-deliberation conversation of 2026-08-27–29 tested the observation
  against the planner's existing categories: not FID, where the character's
  own vocabulary and idiom color the narration; not ZeroFocalization, where
  the narrator states objective world-fact; the content here is the
  character's subjective assessment, but the form is the narrator's own
  literary register. That conversation named the pattern the "narrator-
  character blend" and raised it as a hypothesis, testing whether the ZF/ND
  split has a clean place for it.
