## Hypothesis

Embedded texts and dreams sit outside the standard mechanism categories (M1-M4)
as a distinct structural device that frames reader interpretation differently
from the embedding narrative's own mechanism — the reader processes the embedded
content through one interpretive frame while simultaneously processing its
relationship to the embedding narrative through another.

## Record

- created | 2026-08-31T20:00: The 42-story corpus identified embedded texts
  (letters, journal entries, songs, prophecies, in-world documents) and dream
  sequences as a recurring structural device that the M1-M4 mechanism taxonomy
  could not cleanly categorize. An embedded letter operates through direct
  presentation (M1) within its own frame, but the reader simultaneously performs
  inference about why the author included it, what it reveals about the character
  reading it, and how its content relates to the embedding narrative's fabula.
  The mechanism is not the letter's own presentational mode — it is the act of
  embedding, which creates a double interpretive layer. Dreams present a similar
  problem: their content may use any mechanism, but the reader reads them as
  psychologically revealing (a character's dream tells the reader about the
  character's mind, not about the story's fabula, unless the story's rules say
  otherwise). The hypothesis is that embedding is a structural category of its own
  rather than a modifier on existing mechanisms, and that the framework needs to
  recognize it distinctly rather than forcing embedded content into M1-M4
  classifications that describe only the inner layer.
- evidence | 2026-08-31T23:30 | (WU1.1) [supporting]: Embedded texts are cited
  as a framework gap in ~14% of the short corpus (8 stories) and more
  prominently in the long corpus: letters creating doubled reading positions,
  diaries as access-mode shifts, dream sequences as liminal interiority (3
  dark-premise stories), fantasy sequences as unreliable interiority (2
  explicit/plot stories). The double-interpretive-layer structure is confirmed.
  4.1a's assessment is that embedded texts "do not need a dedicated planning-
  level track" — but this is based on the corpus's narrower use (characterization,
  access-mode shifts). Whether the planning-level significance is higher when
  embedded texts serve as structural scaffolding or dual-function world
  infrastructure is an open question for WU1.3, WU1.4, and WU1.9.
