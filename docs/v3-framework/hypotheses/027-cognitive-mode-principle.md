## Hypothesis

The cognitive mode separation principle (ZeroFocalization vs NarrativeDesign —
"what is true" vs "how the reader encounters truth") is correct as a principle,
but the specific boundaries and mode definitions may need revision to
accommodate the three-level framework and non-inferential goals.

## Record

- created | 2026-08-31T20:00: v2 introduced cognitive modes as the top-level
  organizational split: ZeroFocalization tracks handle fabula (what is true in
  the world — ontology, history, canon compliance), while NarrativeDesign
  tracks handle syuzhet (how the reader encounters that truth — perception
  gaps, prior beliefs, revelation timing). The split maps to the fabula/syuzhet
  distinction in narratology and has proved durable — the real data files use
  it, the track definitions are organized around it, and it reflects a genuine
  cognitive difference in the work Brian does. What the hypothesis questions is
  whether the specific mode boundaries hold under the three-level framework
  (hypothesis 023). Some territory does not fit cleanly: the narrator-character
  blend (hypothesis 030) channels both the narrator's literary register and the
  focalizer's subjective assessment, sitting between ZF's objectivity and ND's
  designed subjectivity. Non-inferential goals like humor or emotional
  resonance (where the reader is not being asked to infer anything, but to feel
  something) may not belong in NarrativeDesign's inference-oriented frame. The
  five-voice separation (prose/craft, fabula, syuzhet/design, Brian's
  analytical, AI analytical) further complicates the picture — fabula and
  syuzhet/design map to ZF and ND, but Brian's analytical voice operates in
  both modes simultaneously. The principle survives; the map may need redrawing.
