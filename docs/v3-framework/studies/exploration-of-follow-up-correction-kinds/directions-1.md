## What you are given

One exchange from an archive of conversations between a person planning a long work of
fiction and an AI model: which layer of the archive it comes from, a label for the
conversation, its date, the ids of the two turns, then the model's turn, and then the user
turn that followed it. A turn is a run of consecutive messages by one side, so either may be
several messages joined. You have nothing else: not the rest of the conversation, not the
data being spoken about, not what either side knew from elsewhere.

## How to read

Read the model turn, then the user turn, and ask whether the user turn corrects the model
turn: says or shows that something in it is wrong, mistaken, out of date, misread or
misdirected. Adding new material, asking a further question, or going somewhere else is not
a correction. Disagreement stated as disagreement is.

Where it corrects, take each correction separately and say what is being corrected. Five
kinds are asked for by name. A fact of the story's world: the model got a name, an event, a
date, a relationship, a piece of the world's history or a character's psychology wrong. The
model's reading of the plan: the model took what the planning material holds to say
something it does not say. Which body of material the model drew on: the model used the
wrong source, an outdated one, or treated something provisional as settled. Register or
format: how the model wrote, how long, in what shape, what it should have produced.
The model's reading of the request: the model misunderstood what was asked of it. Report
every other kind you find as well, named in your own terms.

Then say how the correction is put — flatly, with a reason given, as a question, with an
apology, with irritation, in passing while getting on with something else, or something else
in your own terms. Read only what the turns say; never decide who was right, and never
correct either turn yourself.

## What to produce

- corrects: line, one of yes or no, whether the user turn corrects anything in the model turn before it
- corrections: list of line, one per correction the user turn makes, four parts separated by a bar: kind, one of fact of the world, reading of the plan, which material was drawn on, register or format, reading of the request, or your own name for any other | what is corrected, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the user turn allowed | manner, how the correction is put, in your own terms; empty where the turn corrects nothing
- about: line, what the user turn is doing as a whole, in one sentence

## Never

Never give a position inside a turn: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never judge whether the correction was right. Never say what either turn
should have said.
