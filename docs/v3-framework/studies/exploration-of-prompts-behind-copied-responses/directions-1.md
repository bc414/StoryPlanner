## What you are given

One message a person planning a long work of fiction typed to an AI model: which archive of
conversations it comes from, a label for the conversation, its date, the id of the message,
then the message itself. A message may be several consecutive messages joined; an attached
document that was never captured shows as a placeholder. You have nothing else: not the
model's reply, not the rest of the conversation, not the planning material the message may
speak of.

## How to read

Read the message and describe it as a request. Say each thing it asks the model to do —
analyse, explain, brainstorm, critique, check, choose, draft, rewrite, summarise, continue,
or anything else — naming the kind in your own terms. Say what material it hands the model
to work on: a pasted stretch of the plan, a passage of prose, a document, an earlier reply,
a list, a single idea, or nothing beyond the question. Say every instruction it gives about
the reply itself: its form, its length, its stance, its perspective, what to include or leave
out. Then say how open it is: whether it leaves the answer open, leans toward an answer it
names, asks for a choice between options it names, or asks the model to check something the
message already states. Describe only what the message says; never guess what the reply was
or what became of it.

## What to produce

- asks: list of line, one per thing the message asks the model to do, three parts separated by a bar: kind, your own name for the kind of request | what it asks, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the message allowed
- supplies: list of line, one per body of material the message hands the model, three parts separated by a bar: material, your own name for what it is | holds, what it is about in a few words | how much, a short phrase such as a line, a paragraph, several pages; empty where it hands over nothing
- shaping: list of line, one per instruction about the reply, two parts separated by a bar: instruction, what the message asks of the reply's form, length, stance or content | what marks it, a few words of the message allowed; empty where it gives none
- openness: line, whether the message leaves the answer open, leans toward an answer it names, asks for a choice between named options, or asks the model to check what it states, and what shows it, in one sentence
- subject: line, what the message is about, in a few words

## Never

Never give a position inside the message: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never guess at the reply or at what the person did with it. Never say how
the message should have been written.
