## What you are given

One note carrying a theme tag, from the planning database of a long work of fiction: what
owns it — a subject, a scene, a chapter, or the link between a scene and a subject — the
track it sits in with the question that track asks its notes, the theme it is tagged with
and the proposition the author wrote for that theme, its world date where it has one, then
the note's own text; and after it, the owner's other notes, each under its track name.
Those other notes are context: they are there so that what the owner already records is
visible. They are not the note, and nothing that appears only in them is reported as the
note's content. You have nothing else of the plan: not the other themes, not the other
owners, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read the note, then each thing it says. For each, ask what kind of claim it is. Three kinds
are asked for by name. A reading: what some fact of the story's world means, what it shows,
what a reader or a critic could conclude from it — the fact is taken as given and the note
adds an interpretation of it. A design commitment: something the author is deciding shall
be so — a fact of the world, a character's psychology, an event, a thing that will be on
the page, a thing the reader will be led to — stated as settled rather than read off. A
restatement: the note repeats what the theme's own proposition, or a note beside it, already
says, adding nothing. Report every other kind you find as well, named in your own terms.

For each thing, say what question it answers, in your own words and in one short clause —
the question someone would have had to ask to get that sentence. Then say whether that is
the question this note's own track asks, which the item gives you: yes where it answers it,
no where it answers something else, partly where it answers it along with something else.
Judge only what the words hold, never what the note could be made to hold.

## What to produce

- claims: list of line, one per thing the note says, five parts separated by a bar: kind, one of reading, design commitment, restatement, or your own name for any other | what it says, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed | the question it answers, in one short clause of your own words | fits, one of yes, no or partly, whether that question is the one this note's track asks
- theme: line, how the note stands to the theme it is tagged with: it argues the proposition, it supplies evidence for it, it names the theme without arguing it, it does not touch the theme, or something else in your words

## Never

Never name a position inside the note: no line or sentence numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never report what is in the notes beside it as this note's content.
Never say what the note should have held, and never name a track other than the one the
item gives you.
