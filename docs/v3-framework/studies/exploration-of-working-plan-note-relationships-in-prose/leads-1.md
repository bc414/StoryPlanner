## Leads

### exploration-of-working-plan-note-relationships-in-prose/leads-1/how-many-joints-the-prose-carries
- lead: Over 351 of the plan's 353 note-owning owners the readers found 2,293 joints between two of that
  owner's notes — a median of 6 per owner, 1 at the least and 56 at the most. Of those, 2,111 are implicit,
  carried only by what the two notes say, and 178 are explicit, where one note points at the other in
  words. So in the working plan roughly twelve joints in thirteen name neither end: the relationships are
  there in the prose and almost nothing in the prose points at them.
- seen in: owners of every kind in the working plan — subjects, scenes, chapters and scene links

### exploration-of-working-plan-note-relationships-in-prose/leads-1/what-kinds-of-joint-the-prose-carries
- lead: Under the loose alternations the commonest joint is one note continuing another in time: 382 lines
  over 138 owners. Then one giving the cause or reason of what another states, 341 lines over 147 owners;
  one being an instance or occasion of what another states generally, 254 lines over 143; one stating the
  reader's response to what another states, 143 lines over 76; one revising, narrowing, overturning or
  complicating another, 135 lines over 96; one planning the delivery on the page of what another states as
  true, 116 lines over 82; one presupposing another and not working without it, 108 lines over 84; and two
  contradicting each other or standing in tension, 54 lines over 41. The families overlap where a reader
  named a joint with more than one of these words.

### exploration-of-working-plan-note-relationships-in-prose/leads-1/a-third-of-owners-hold-only-one-note
- lead: Read against the index's description column, 109 of the 353 owners hold a single note, so there is
  nothing for their notes to join; 86 hold two or three, 96 hold four to ten, and 62 hold more than ten.
  The 353 owners hold 2,095 notes between them. The readers said so in the whole line for those owners:
  "the owner holds a single note, so there is nothing for it to hang together with, and it stands as one
  entry."

### exploration-of-working-plan-note-relationships-in-prose/leads-1/subjects-hang-together-and-scenes-do-not
- lead: Sorting the whole line by the kind of owner: of the 211 subjects, 117 were read as hanging together
  as one set, 51 as a loose chain or clusters with notes that join none of the others, and 29 hold a single
  note. Of the 89 scene links, 47 hold a single note and 34 hang together. Of the 39 plot points, 26 hold a
  single note and only 5 hang together. Of the 14 chapters, 8 hold a single note. So the joints the prose
  carries live mostly on the subjects; the scene-level owners mostly hold one note each and have nothing to
  join.

### exploration-of-working-plan-note-relationships-in-prose/leads-1/loose-chains-that-the-notes-do-not-draw
- lead: Where a subject's notes were read as a loose chain, the reader's reason is usually that the notes
  are in an order the content implies and nothing states: three notes running from a standard language to
  the first translators to their later exploitation, "though the link between 1821 and 1808 is not drawn
  and each note mostly stands on its own"; a currency analogy and a historian's account of the same
  money-like nature, "though neither points at the other"; a subject whose notes fall into clusters — a
  real-world analogy, an industrial causation, a suppression mechanism, and a reader's reveal — with some
  notes joining none of the others.

## Proposed questions

- Where two notes of one owner are joined only by content, which pairs of tracks do they sit in, and are
  the same track pairs joined again and again?
- Of the working plan's owners that hold a single note, how many are scene links, and does a scene link
  with one note mean the scene's design is held somewhere else?
- Where a subject's notes were read as a loose chain in an order the content implies, does the plan's
  SortOrder already put them in that order?
- Does an explicit joint in the working plan — one note naming another — occur on any particular track, or
  is it scattered?
- Where a note speaks of something its owner does not hold, does the plan hold that thing as a subject, and
  would a subject relation have carried it?
