## What you are given

One owner from the planning database of a long work of fiction — a subject, a scene, a
chapter, or the link between a scene and a subject — and every note that hangs off it, each
under its own note id with the track it sits in, the question that track asks its notes, its
theme tag and its world date where it has them, and its text. The database records no link
between one note and another: the only thing that could join two notes is what their words
say. You have nothing else of the plan: not the other owners' notes, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read all of this owner's notes, then read them against each other. Look for the places where
one note and another are joined by what they say — where the two are not merely about the
same owner, which they all are, but where one bears on the other.

Kinds of joint to look for, and there will be others: one gives the cause or the reason of
what the other states; one is an instance or an occasion of what the other states generally;
one revises, narrows or overturns what the other says; one presupposes the other to be true
and does not work without it; one plans the delivery on the page of what the other states as
true; one states the reader's response to what the other states; one continues the other in
time; two contradict each other. Name the kind in your own terms.

Say for each whether the joint is explicit — one note points at the other in words, by
naming it, by "as above", "see", a shared proper name introduced in the other, or a
numbered reference — or implicit, where nothing points and only the content joins them. An
implicit joint must be one a reader of these two notes alone would make; do not join two
notes on knowledge of the work from outside this item.

Separately, note where a note plainly speaks of something that is not in this item at all —
another character, another scene, another story, a piece of lore held elsewhere — since that
is a joint whose other end is not here.

## What to produce

- relations: list of line, one per joint between two of this owner's notes, five parts separated by a bar: one, the id of the first note as the item gives it | other, the id of the second | kind, what joins them, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the notes allowed | strength, one of explicit where a note points at the other in words, or implicit where only the content joins them; empty where the notes hold none
- outward: list of line, one per place a note plainly speaks of something not in this item, three parts separated by a bar: note, the id as the item gives it | what it points at, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed; empty where there are none
- whole: line, whether this owner's notes read as a set that hangs together or as separate entries, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside a note beyond the note's own id: no line or sentence numbers,
and no quotation used only to say where. Never join two notes on anything but their own
words. Never say what a note should have held, and never propose a link the plan ought to
record.
