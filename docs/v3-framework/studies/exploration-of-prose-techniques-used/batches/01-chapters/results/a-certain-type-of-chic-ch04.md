- techniques:
  - Close third-person internal focalization | narration tracks only Twilight's perceptions and inferences about others ('must have', 'seemed to', 'looked to have') | throughout the chapter, both the station scene and the boutique/library scenes
  - Free indirect discourse | unmarked shift into a character's own phrasing and rhetorical questions within third-person narration ('Am I being obvious?', 'Why is she asking that?', string of 'Was this...?' questions) | during Twilight's walk to the boutique and inside the fitting scene
  - Italicized direct-thought insertions | short present-tense or imperative clauses set off in italics ('One more time,' 'This will be the last time,' 'No, that would just be too easy,' 'Maybe I'm over-thinking things,' the closing 'That...truly was the magic of friendship') | scattered through the apple-spell scene, the walk to the boutique, and the fitting-room scene, plus the station ending
  - Scene-break divider with gnomic opening | a row of asterisks followed by a proverb-like generalizing sentence about time and seeds | marks the jump from the station homecoming to the library/apple-spell scene
  - Compressed summary narration | a paragraph glossing over 'a week or two' of repeated visits in indirect, generalized past tense rather than scene | opens the second section before the scene narrows to a single day
  - Dramatic irony via question exchange | Rarity's pointed questions about the dress ('What did you say happened, again?') read as suspicious to the reader before Twilight consciously registers it | the button/dress dialogue in the workroom
  - Somatic metaphor for emotion | bodily/physical figures used to render feeling ('heart thudded against her ribs,' 'suddenly weightless,' 'stomach moved in strange ways,' 'hollow ache in her chest') | the kiss scene and its aftermath at the library
  - Staccato short-sentence fragments for urgency | clipped declarative fragments stacked without connectives ('There was no choice now. There was no backing out... It was now or never. All or nothing.') | immediately before Twilight kisses Rarity
  - Overheard/relayed dialogue | a conversation reported as heard through a wall/door by the point-of-view character rather than witnessed directly, interspersed with her internal reaction | Spike and Rarity's exchange at the library door while Twilight listens from her bedroom
  - Epithet substitution for character names | recurring descriptive noun phrases used in place of proper names ('the grey unicorn,' 'the light grey mare,' 'her erstwhile lover') | across the kiss and its immediate aftermath
  - Rhetorical-question cascades | strings of unanswered questions posed in a character's internal deliberation to render indecision | Twilight's pacing outside the boutique and again after the button-scene realization
  - Paratextual author's note | direct authorial address to the reader outside the story's fictional frame, thanking named commenters and discussing revision choices | appended after the chapter's final scene
