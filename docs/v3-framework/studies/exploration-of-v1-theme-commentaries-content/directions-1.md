## What you are given

One commentary the author wrote on the link between a plot point and a theme, from the
planning database of a long work of fiction: the plot point's title, its chapter and
position, the theme's name and the description the author wrote for that theme, the plot
point's synopsis, then the commentary itself, with the settings chosen on the link in
brackets where it has any. The settings are labels picked from fixed lists; they say what
the author selected, not what the scene does. You have nothing else of the plan: not the
other plot points, not the theme's other commentaries, not the linked entities' own notes.
The synopsis is given so that content the commentary repeats from it is visible. The
synopsis is not the commentary, and nothing that appears only in the synopsis is reported
as the commentary's content.

## How to read

Read the commentary whole, then passage by passage, a passage being a stretch of it that
does one thing. A commentary may hold several passages of different kinds, and one passage
may hold more than one kind at once. Five kinds are asked for by name. A statement of the
scene's bearing on the theme: what this scene does for the theme — carries it, tests it,
complicates it, exemplifies it, argues it. Fabula content: something true in the story's
world — its history, its laws, an institution, a character's psychology, a relationship —
stated as fact, whether or not this scene shows it. An on-page plan: what is to be on the
page — a line of dialogue, an image, an action, a staging, an order of events. A note to
self: a task, a doubt, a question left open, a reminder, a decision still to be made. A
note about the reader: what the reader is to feel, believe, infer, be led to or be
corrected about. Report every other kind you find as well, named in your own terms. Judge
only what the words hold, never what the scene could be made to hold, and never what the
commentary ought to have held.

## What to produce

- passages: list of line, one per passage of the commentary, five parts separated by a bar: kind, one of bearing on the theme, fabula content, on-page plan, note to self, note about the reader, or your own name for any other | what the passage holds, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the commentary allowed | repeated, yes where the passage restates something the synopsis already says and no where it does not | register, how the passage is written, such as planning shorthand, a directive, expository prose, a question, a list, and the few words that show it
- whole: line, what the commentary is as a whole, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside the commentary: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation
used only to say where. Never report what is in the synopsis alone as the commentary's
content. Never say what the commentary should have held.
