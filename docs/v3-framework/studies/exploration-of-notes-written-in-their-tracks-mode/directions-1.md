## What you are given

One note from the planning database of a long work of fiction: what owns it — a subject, a
scene, a chapter, or the link between a scene and a subject — the track it sits in, the
track's type and the mode that type declares, the display question the track asks its notes,
the theme it is tagged with and its world date where it has them, then the note's own text.
You have nothing else of the plan: not the owner's other notes, not the other tracks, not
any directive the track carries beyond its display question, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read the note, then each thing it says, a thing being a stretch that does one job; a
sentence may hold more than one, and one thing may run across sentences.

For each thing, two readings, made apart. First, whether it is written in the mode the
track's type declares, judged by what its words do; where it is not, what it is written as
instead, in your own words. Second, whether it gives part of an answer to the track's display
question, read as it is worded. A thing may be in the track's mode and answer nothing the
question asks, or be outside the mode and answer it.

Then read the display question against the whole note: each part of what the question asks
that nothing in the note's words answers.

Judge only what the words hold, never what the note could be made to hold.

## What to produce

- claims: list of line, one per thing the note says, four parts separated by a bar: what it says, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed | mode, yes where it is written in the mode the track's type declares, or no followed by what it is written as instead, in your words | answers, yes where the thing gives part of an answer to the display question, no where it does not
- unanswered: list of line, one per part of the display question that nothing in the note answers, the part in your words; empty where the note answers every part
- whole: line, what this note is doing, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside the note: no line or sentence numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never judge a thing to be in the track's mode, or to answer its question,
because it sits in that track; judge it by what the words do. Never say what the note should
have held, where it should have gone, or whether the track, its type or its question is well
made.
