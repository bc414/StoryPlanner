## What you are given

One plot point from the archived planning database of a long work of fiction: its title, its
chapter and position, its synopsis, and its outcome and stakes where they hold text; then
every link it has, to a character, a theme, a story thread or a codex entry of world lore,
each under its kind and name, with the settings the author chose on it in brackets and the
link's own text, "(no text)" where it has none. The synopsis and each link's text are
free-form boxes the author wrote into over time. You have nothing else of the plan: not the
other plot points, not the linked entities' own notes, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read the whole item, the links' text as well as the synopsis. Look for the places where it
instructs, rather than reports — where it says what the finished page is to do, not what
happens in the scene.

Two kinds are asked for by name. Planting: the page is to put something there so the reader
picks it up, works it out, or is set up for something later — a detail to show, a hint to
drop, a thing the reader is to infer, notice or conclude. Withholding: the page is not to
say something, or is to say it only obliquely, or the character whose side the scene is seen
from is not to realize, understand or register it — a fact held back, an explanation
deferred, a reaction left unexplained. Marks of either are phrases such as "the narrative
doesn't mention", "shouldn't fully understand", "the reader should", "don't reveal", "only
hint", "leave it unsaid", "plant", "set up", "foreshadow", "save for later", a "should" or a
"must" about what is on the page, and a parenthetical aside about what the audience is to
take away. Report every other kind of instruction about the page you find as well, named in
your own terms.

For each, name what it points at: the fact of the story's world that is to be inferred, or
that is being kept back, as the item gives it; where the item does not give it, say so. Also
say how the passage reads — planning shorthand, a directive, a parenthetical aside, a
question, expository prose — and the few words that show it, saying nothing about who wrote
it. Report only what the words instruct, never what the scene could be made to instruct.

## What to produce

- directives: list of line, one per instruction about what the page does, six parts separated by a bar: kind, one of planting, withholding, or your own name for any other | what it directs, in your words | what it points at, the fact of the story's world to be inferred or kept back; not given where the item does not give it | what marks it, a few words of the item allowed | register, how the passage reads, and nothing about who wrote it | where in the item, the section or the link by its kind and name; empty where the item holds none
- reports: line, whether this item speaks of the reader or the audience at all, and in what way, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside a box: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used only
to say where. Never judge whose words a passage is in. Never count a plain statement of what
happens in the scene as an instruction about the page. Never say what the item should have
held.
