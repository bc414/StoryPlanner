## What you are given

One subject from the archived planning database of a long work of fiction — a character, a
place, a piece of world lore, an organization or the like: its name, the label it was given
in a later review pass, its description where it has one, then every note that hangs off it,
each under its own note id. The review label is a queue marker from that pass; it says how
the subject was to be handled, not what kind of thing it is. You have nothing else of the
plan: not the scenes, not the other subjects, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read each note in turn, then each passage of it, a passage being a stretch that does one
thing. Ask of each passage what it is. Some passages are beats of a scene: something
happening at a moment — someone does something, says something, arrives, discovers, decides
— written as an event the story could show. Some of those carry a year or a date or a span
of the story world's calendar, in the passage or in a heading above it; some carry none.
Other passages are statements about the subject: what it is, what it is like, what is true
of it, what it means, a rule it follows — not a moment. Report every other kind you find as
well, named in your own terms.

Where a note runs as a sequence — several beats one after another, in an order that reads as
the order they happen in — say so, and say what marks the sequence: years counting up, a
list, headings, connectives such as "then", "later", "after". Read only what the words hold.
Never date a passage from what you know of the world outside the item.

## What to produce

- passages: list of line, one per passage, five parts separated by a bar: note, the note's id as the item gives it | kind, one of scene beat with a year, scene beat without a year, statement about the subject, or your own name for any other | what it holds, in your words | the year, date or span it carries, in the words the passage uses; none where it carries none | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed
- sequences: list of line, one per note that runs as a sequence of beats in order, three parts separated by a bar: note, the note's id as the item gives it | how many beats it runs and what the sequence covers, in your words | what marks the sequence, a few words of the note allowed; empty where no note does
- whole: line, what this subject's notes are as a set, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside a note beyond the note's own id: no line or paragraph numbers,
and no quotation used only to say where. Never say what a note should have held, and never
judge whose words a note is in.
