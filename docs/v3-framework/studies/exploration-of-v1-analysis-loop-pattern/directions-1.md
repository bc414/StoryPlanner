## What you are given

One conversation, or one consecutive part of a long one, between a person planning a long
work of fiction and an AI model: which archive it comes from, a label, its date, then every
message in order under its id, marked as the author's or the model's. An attached document
that was never captured shows as a placeholder. Under some messages is a list of notes from
the author's planning database whose text a mechanical comparison traces to that message,
each with where in the database it sits and how its text relates to the message: pasted
whole or with cuts, pasted inside the author's own framing, one sentence lifted, the
author's own words, or text the planning database held before the reply quoted it back. A
message with no list has no note traced to it; that does not mean nothing came of it. You
have nothing else: not the rest of the planning database, not other conversations.

## How to read

Read the conversation from beginning to end as a piece of work, and describe how the work
went, at the level of what each side did rather than what the story says. What did the author
bring in — part of the plan, a question, an idea, a draft, a correction? What did the model
return — an analysis, options, a draft, a verdict, questions? How did the author respond — take
it up, redirect, correct, ask for more, move on? Name each move in your own terms and keep them
in the order they happen.

Then read the notes traced to the messages and say what the planning database kept of the
conversation: which messages it drew on, how — whole, cut, framed, a sentence, the author's
own words — and where the kept text went. Finally say what loop this conversation shows,
from what the author brought to what the plan kept. Describe the work, not the story: say
that a character was analysed, not what the analysis concluded about the character.

## What to produce

- steps: list of line, one per move in the conversation, in order, four parts separated by a bar: who, the author or the model | move, your own name for what the move does | carries, what it carries in a few words | where in the conversation, in words
- kept: list of line, one per note traced to a message, three parts separated by a bar: note, the note's id as the item gives it | relation, how its text relates to its message, as the item says | keeps, what of the conversation the note keeps and where in the database it went, in a few words; empty where no note is traced
- brought: line, what the author brought into the conversation from the plan or from elsewhere, in one sentence; none where nothing was brought
- loop: line, the working loop this conversation shows, from what the author brought to what the plan kept, in one or two sentences

## Never

Never give a position by number: no message counts or line numbers, and no quotation used only
to say where. Never report the story's content as the finding; report what was done with it.
Never say how the work should have gone.
