## What you are given

One plot point from the archived planning database of a long work of fiction: its title, its
chapter and position, its synopsis, and its outcome and stakes where they hold text; then
every link it has, to a character, a theme, a story thread or a codex entry of world lore,
each under its kind and name, with the settings the author chose on it in brackets and the
link's own text, "(no text)" where it has none. A character link says the author attached
that character to this scene; it does not say the character is on the page, and it does not
say whose side the scene is seen from. You have nothing else of the plan: not the other plot
points, not the linked entities' own notes, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read the whole item, the links' text as well as the synopsis. Two things are asked.

First, who is in the scene: every character the item's words show as present — doing
something, saying something, being addressed, being somewhere. A character the item only
mentions, or whose link carries no sign of them being there, is not present; say that
separately where the item names them.

Second, whose perception the scene is written from: the character whose noticing, feeling,
judging or not-knowing the text follows. Marks of it are phrases such as "she sees", "he
doesn't realize", "from her point of view", "the reader learns with", a reaction given from
one side only, or a fact withheld that one character would not know. Where the item's words
show none of this, the answer is undetermined. Do not settle it from who is linked, from
who is named most, from who the story is about, or from anything you know of the work
outside this item: an undetermined scene is a legitimate and common answer.

## What to produce

- present: list of line, one per character the item shows as present in the scene, three parts separated by a bar: character, as the item names them | what shows they are there, a few words of the item allowed | where in the item, the section or the link by its kind and name
- mentioned: list of line, one per character the item names but does not show as present, two parts separated by a bar: character, as the item names them | where in the item; empty where there are none
- focalizer: line, the character whose perception the item's text is written from, as the item names them; undetermined where the item's words show none
- shows: line, the few words of the item that show the focalizer; none where undetermined
- sides: list of line, one per passage that gives someone's noticing, feeling or judging from their own side, three parts separated by a bar: character, whose it is | what it is, in your words | where in the item, the section or the link by its kind and name; empty where there are none

## Never

Never name a position inside a box: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used only
to say where. Never infer who is present or whose side the scene is seen from anything but
the item's own words. Never say what the item should have held.
