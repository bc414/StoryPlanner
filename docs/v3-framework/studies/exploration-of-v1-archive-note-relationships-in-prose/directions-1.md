## What you are given

One plot point from the archived planning database of a long work of fiction: its title, its
chapter and position, its synopsis, and its outcome and stakes where they hold text; then
every link it has, to a character, a theme, a story thread or a codex entry of world lore,
each under its kind and name, with the settings the author chose on it in brackets and the
link's own text, "(no text)" where it has none. The settings are labels picked from fixed
lists; they say what the author selected, not what the scene does. Each of these boxes —
the synopsis, the outcome, the stakes, each link's text — is a free-form box the author
wrote into over time, and the database records no link between one box and another: the only
thing that could join two of them is what their words say. You have nothing else of the
plan: not the other plot points, not the linked entities' own notes, not the story's text.

## How to read

Read the whole item, the links' text as well as the synopsis, then read the boxes against
each other. Look for the places where one box and another are joined by what they say —
where the two are not merely attached to the same scene, which they all are, but where one
bears on the other.

Kinds of joint to look for, and there will be others: one gives the cause or the reason of
what the other states; one is an instance or an occasion of what the other states generally;
one revises, narrows or overturns what the other says; one presupposes the other to be true
and does not work without it; one plans the delivery on the page of what the other states as
true; one states the reader's response to what the other states; one continues the other in
time; two contradict each other.

Say for each whether the joint is explicit — one box points at the other in words, by naming
the character, thread or codex entry the other box belongs to, by "as above", "see", or a
shared proper name introduced in the other — or implicit, where nothing points and only the
content joins them. An implicit joint must be one a reader of these two boxes alone would
make; do not join two boxes on knowledge of the work from outside this item.

Separately, note where a box plainly speaks of something not in this item at all — another
scene, another chapter, a character or a piece of lore with no link here — since that is a
joint whose other end is not here.

## What to produce

- relations: list of line, one per joint between two boxes of this item, five parts separated by a bar: one, the first box as the item names it, the section or the link by its kind and name | other, the second box the same way | kind, what joins them, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the item allowed | strength, one of explicit where a box points at the other in words, or implicit where only the content joins them; empty where the item holds none
- outward: list of line, one per place a box plainly speaks of something not in this item, three parts separated by a bar: box, as the item names it | what it points at, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the item allowed; empty where there are none
- whole: line, whether this item's boxes read as one design that hangs together or as separate entries, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside a box: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used only
to say where. Never join two boxes on anything but their own words. Never say what a box
should have held, and never propose a link the plan ought to record.
