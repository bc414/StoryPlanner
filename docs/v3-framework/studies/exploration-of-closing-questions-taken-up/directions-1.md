## What you are given

One exchange from an archive of conversations between a person planning a long work of
fiction and an AI model: which layer of the archive it comes from, a label for the
conversation, its date, the ids of the two turns, then the model's turn, whose last
paragraph holds a question, and then the user turn that followed it, or "(none: the
conversation ends here)" where the conversation ended there. A turn is a run of consecutive
messages by one side, so either may be several messages joined. You have nothing else: not
the rest of the conversation, not the planning database, not what was written afterwards.

## How to read

Read the model turn to its end, and find the questions it puts to the user — the ones asked
of the user to answer or decide, not questions the model asks and then answers itself, and
not rhetorical ones. Take each separately, even where several sit in one sentence.

Then read the user turn and ask, of each question, what became of it. It may be answered —
the turn says something that settles it. Partly answered — the turn takes up part of it, or
answers with a condition, or narrows it. Refused — the turn declines to settle it, defers
it, or says the question is wrong. Ignored — the turn goes on to something else and never
touches it. Where there is no user turn, that is a fifth answer.

Then say what the user turn does as a whole: answers, redirects to something else, corrects
the model, gives an instruction, ends the conversation, or something else in your own
terms. And say whether anything the user turn settles is a decision about the work itself —
a fact of the story's world, a structure, a name, a plan — as against a decision about the
conversation, such as what to look at next. Read only what the turns say.

## What to produce

- questions: list of line, one per question the model turn puts to the user, four parts separated by a bar: question, in your words | outcome, one of answered, partly answered, refused, ignored, or no user turn | what the user turn says to it, in your words; none where it says nothing to it | what marks it, a few words of the user turn allowed; none where there is no user turn
- shape: line, what the user turn does as a whole, in your own terms; none where there is no user turn
- settles: list of line, one per decision about the work itself that the user turn settles, two parts separated by a bar: what is settled, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the user turn allowed; empty where it settles none

## Never

Never give a position inside a turn: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never say what the user turn should have answered, and never supply an
answer the turns do not hold.
