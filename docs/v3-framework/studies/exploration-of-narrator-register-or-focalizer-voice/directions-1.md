## What you are given

One chapter of a story: the story's slug, the chapter's number and title, then the chapter's
text as the file holds it, italics as *...*. You have nothing else of the story: not the
other chapters, not the author's notes, not any analysis of it.

## How to read

Read the whole chapter, paragraph by paragraph. Find the stretches of narration — not
quoted dialogue — that render what a character perceives, notices, feels or judges: a
description coloured by how it strikes someone, a judgment nobody is speaking aloud, an
observation given from inside someone's head.

For each such stretch, ask whose perception it renders, and then ask in whose words it is
carried. Two answers are asked for by name. The character's own words: the wording is one
that character uses, or plainly would use — their vocabulary, their idiom, their way of
naming things, their register. A narrator's words: the wording is one that character would
not use — a figure, a word, a cadence or a knowingness that belongs to a voice standing
apart from them. Mixed where one stretch does both. Say what makes you answer as you do,
in a few words of the page.

Then, for each stretch, say whether wording of the same kind appears in that character's own
speech or thought elsewhere in this chapter: yes where it does, no where it does not, and
none where that character neither speaks nor thinks in words anywhere in this chapter.
Decide this from this chapter alone; say nothing about the character from any other text.

Separately, say whether this chapter has a narrating voice distinct from every character's —
one that knows, judges or addresses in a way no character in the chapter does — and what
marks it.

## What to produce

- renderings: list of line, one per stretch of narration that renders what a character perceives or judges, six parts separated by a bar: character, whose perception it renders, as the chapter names them | carrier, one of the character's own words, a narrator's words, or mixed | what it renders, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the page allowed | echoed, one of yes, no, or none where the character neither speaks nor thinks in this chapter | where in the chapter, in words
- narrator: line, whether this chapter has a narrating voice distinct from every character's, and what marks it; none where it has not
- whole: line, how this chapter's narration stands to its characters' perception, in one sentence

## Never

Never give a position: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used only to say
where. Never judge a character's vocabulary from any text but this chapter. Never say what
the prose should have done, and never name the author.
