## What you are given

One subject from the planning database of a long work of fiction: a character, a bond, an
organization, a system, a technology or a world law. Two parts. First, its subject-wide notes.
Second, its scene-link notes, each written about this subject in one scene, with the scene named.
In each part every track the subject can hold is listed, even an empty one. Each track shows its
name, its type, the mode that type declares, and the display question it asks its notes. A note
shows its id, its theme and world date where it has them, and its text. A few notes sit under
"track: none (unassigned)".

Twelve track types, each declaring one mode:
- Ontology: written by a world builder in god-mode defining what the rules of the fictional universe are
- Civilization: written by a world builder building things made by in-universe agents in response to their ontology
- History: written by an in-universe historian reporting facts
- Characterization: written by a psychologist asserting the truth of what makes a character who they are
- PageDesign: written by a director staging what the reader observes on the page
- WorldInference: written by a reader psychologist designing what a reader should infer from what is on the page
- ThematicEvidence: written by a philosopher deploying evidence for a reader to arrive at a thematic proposition
- NotesToSelf: written by the author, talking about the story planning project and process
- Analogies: written by the author to document real-world inspiration (can be historical or present-day inspiration)
- Allegories: written by the author as a reader inference target for social commentary
- NarrativeArchitecture: written by the author planning out how the reader should experience the story world
- Canon: written by the author to constrain the story within established canon, build upon and recontextualize canon

You have nothing else of the plan: not the scenes' own notes, not other subjects, not any
directive a track carries beyond its display question, not the story's text. Some of this
subject's notes are withheld from you, so a track shown empty may not be empty in the plan.

## How to read

Read the whole subject first, every track, so that you know what each track on it holds and
which are empty. Then take each note in turn, and each thing it says, a thing being a stretch
that does one job. A sentence may hold more than one thing, and one thing may run across
sentences.

For each thing, three readings, made apart:
- the mode it is written in, of the twelve, judged by what its words do, or outside all twelve;
- whether that is the mode of the track it sits in;
- where the mode is not its track's, whether a track on this subject whose type declares that
  mode, in either part, holds the same content in another note.

Then read the subject whole: which tracks hold content in modes other than their own, from which
modes, and where the same content sits in two tracks.

Judge only what the words hold, never what the note could be made to hold.

## What to produce

- claims: list of line, one per thing a note says, five parts separated by a bar and carrying no labels: the note's id | the track it sits in, by name, or none | what it says, in your words | the mode it is written in, exactly one of the twelve type names, or outside: followed by what it is written as, in your words | where its mode is not its track's, the id of another note on this subject holding the same content in a track of that mode, or exactly none; where its mode is its track's, exactly same
- whole: block, what the subject's tracks show together: which hold content in modes not their own, from which modes, and where the same content sits in two tracks

## Never

Never place a thing in a mode because of the track it sits in, or because of how this subject's
other tracks are used. Place it by what its words do. Never say what a note should have held,
where content should go, whether tracks should be merged or split, or whether a track, its
type or its question is well made. Never name a position inside a note: no line or sentence
numbers, and no quotation used only to say where.
