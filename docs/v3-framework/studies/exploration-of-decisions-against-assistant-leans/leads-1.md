# exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans — leads

## Leads

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/how-the-relation-divides-over-the-sample
- lead: Over 698 of the batch's 2,777 exchanges the readers named the relation between what the assistant's
  turn leaned toward and what the user turn decided. 213 exchanges hold no decision — the user turn is a
  question back, a remark or more material — and 114 hold no lean, the assistant having set out options
  without favouring one or asked open-endedly. Of the 371 where both are present: 149 took the lean, 68 took
  it with a change, 21 took another of the options the assistant set out, 99 took something the assistant did
  not set out at all, and 32 rejected without settling on anything. So where a comparison can be made, the
  decision took the assistant's lean unchanged in two cases in five, and in one case in four took something
  that was not among the options offered. The execution called items in one shuffle of the index, so the 698
  are a random sample of the 2,777.

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/the-three-kinds-of-user-turn-behave-differently
- lead: Read against the kind in the index's description column, the sample holds 602 ordinary typed
  messages, 68 answers to a multiple-choice question the assistant put, and 28 verdicts on a proposed plan.
  Of the 602 prompts: 119 took the lean, 58 with a change, 19 another option, 83 something not set out, 18
  rejected without settling, 100 no lean, 205 no decision. Of the 68 answers: 24 took the lean, 10 with a
  change, 2 another option, 13 something not set out, 3 rejected, 11 no lean, 5 no decision. Of the 28
  verdicts: 6 took the lean, 1 with a change, 4 something not set out, 11 rejected without settling, 3 no
  lean, 3 no decision. So a verdict on a proposed plan is rejected without settling in half the cases, where
  an answer to a posed question takes the lean in about half.

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/what-taking-something-not-set-out-looks-like
- lead: The 99 exchanges where the decision took something the assistant had not set out fall into a few
  recognisable moves. Asking for a test before deciding: against a lean to create a hypothesis with a
  proposed statement, "neither approved nor rejected the statement; instead directed the assistant to test it
  first against a random sample of 10 passages". Correcting the state of play: against a lean to run a pilot
  on one chapter, the answer that the pilot had already been run on a different one. Setting the question
  aside until a dependency is fixed: against a lean on a term, "'codebook' is also too unfamiliar, wants to
  change that term first, then return to this term". Rejecting a proposed narrowing and setting a simpler
  statement instead. Turning a yes-or-no into a cost question: "asks what it would cost to also make
  hypotheses and fanout follow the same governed-folder pattern". Rejecting the framing rather than the
  option: told the assistant "to stop labelling cells with Q and a number because the cells are not a linear
  progression". Reversing what serves what: designing a view from first principles and reworking the data to
  serve it, "sample data is only evidence, not a constraint".

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/a-reason-is-given-in-a-quarter-of-the-decisions
- lead: The grounds field records the reason the user turn gives for its choice in its own words, or none. A
  reason is given in 31 of the 68 multiple-choice answers, 151 of the 602 prompts, and 6 of the 28 verdicts.
  So a posed question draws a stated reason about half the time and an ordinary message about a quarter of the
  time. Where a reason is given it is usually about cost or coherence rather than preference — "reducing
  future reworks is a massive plus; file moves that feel inconvenient now are negligible compared to more
  inconvenience later"; "the history view is bespoke UI filling the gap left by the v1-to-v2 split, so the
  date metadata serves it, not the reverse".

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/a-third-of-exchanges-hold-no-decision-to-compare

### exploration-of-decisions-against-assistant-leans/the-relation-over-all-2777-exchanges
- lead: Over the whole batch, every one of the 2,777 exchanges answered: 914 hold no decision and 481 no lean.
  Of the 1,381 where both are present, 607 took the lean (44%), 254 took it with a change (18%), 79 took
  another option the assistant set out (6%), 315 took something the assistant did not set out (23%), and 126
  rejected without settling (9%). The sample's picture holds at full count: the lean is taken unchanged in a
  little under half the comparable exchanges, and something outside the offered options is chosen in nearly a
  quarter.

## Proposed questions

- Where a decision took something the assistant had not set out, how often was it a request for a test before
  deciding, and did the test then run?
- Do the exchanges where the decision took the lean differ from the others by what was at stake — a name, a
  file layout, a method, a statement of a hypothesis?
- A plan verdict is rejected without settling in half the cases: what did the rejected plans have in common?
- Where the assistant set out options without favouring one, did the decision come faster or slower than
  where it leaned?
- Of the reasons the user turns give, how many are about the cost of future rework, and does that reason
  appear more often as the buildout goes on?
