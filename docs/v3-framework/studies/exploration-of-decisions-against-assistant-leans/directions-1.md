## What you are given

One exchange from a transcript archive of working sessions between a person and an AI
assistant building software and a planning method: a label for the session, its date, what
kind of user turn it is — an answer to a multiple-choice question the assistant put, a
verdict on a plan the assistant proposed, or an ordinary typed message — then the
assistant's turn before it, which is the assistant's text since the previous user turn with
its question and plan calls kept, and then the user turn. You have nothing else: not the
rest of the session, not the code, not what happened afterwards. Where the assistant's turn
records a multiple-choice question, the options themselves are not recorded: only the
question's marker and whatever the assistant's own text says about them.

## How to read

Read the assistant's turn and ask what it leaned toward: the option it recommended, the
course it argued for, the answer it plainly expected, the thing it said it would do next.
A lean may be stated outright, carried by the order or length of what it sets out, or marked
by a word such as recommended, I suggest, I would, the simplest, better. Where it set out
options without favouring one, and where it asked open-endedly, there is no lean; say so.

Then read the user turn and ask what it decided: the option taken, the instruction given,
the plan approved or rejected, the course set. Where it decides nothing — a question back, a
remark, more material — say so.

Then say how the two stand to each other: the decision took the lean; took it with a change;
took another of the options the assistant set out; took something the assistant did not set
out at all; rejected without settling on anything; or there was no lean, or no decision, to
compare. Where the user turn gives a reason for its choice, record the reason in its own
words. Read only what the turns say; never judge who was right.

## What to produce

- lean: line, what the assistant's turn leaned toward, in your words; none where it leaned toward nothing
- decision: line, what the user turn decided, in your words; none where it decided nothing
- relation: line, one of took the lean, took it with a change, took another option set out, took something not set out, rejected without settling, no lean, or no decision
- marks: line, the few words of the assistant's turn and of the user turn that show the lean and the decision; none where there is neither
- grounds: line, the reason the user turn gives for its choice, in the turn's own words; none where it gives none
- subject: line, what the exchange is about, in one short clause

## Never

Never give a position inside a turn: no line or paragraph numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never judge whether the decision or the lean was right, and never say
what either side should have chosen. Never invent options the assistant's turn does not set
out.
