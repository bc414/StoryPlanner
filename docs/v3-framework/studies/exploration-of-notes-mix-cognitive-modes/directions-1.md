## What you are given

One note from the planning database of a long work of fiction: what owns it — a subject, a
scene, a chapter, or the link between a scene and a subject — the track it sits in, the
track's type and the mode that type declares, the question the track asks its notes, the
theme it is tagged with and its world date where it has them, then the note's own text. You
have nothing else of the plan: not the owner's other notes, not the story's text, not the
other tracks' notes.

Every track in this plan has one of ten types, and each type declares the mode its notes are
written in:

- History — written by an in-universe historian reporting facts
- Characterization — written by a psychologist asserting the truth of what makes a character who they are
- PageDesign — written by a director staging what the reader observes on the page
- WorldInference — written by a reader psychologist designing what a reader should infer from what is on the page
- ThematicEvidence — written by a philosopher deploying evidence for a reader to arrive at a thematic proposition
- NotesToSelf — written by the author, talking about the story planning project and process
- Analogies — written by the author to document real-world inspiration, historical or present-day
- NarrativeArchitecture — written by the author planning out how the reader should experience the story world
- Canon — written by the author to constrain the story within established canon, build upon and recontextualize canon
- Allegories — written by the author as a reader inference target for social commentary

## How to read

Read the note, then each thing it says, a thing being a stretch that does one job; a
sentence may hold more than one, and one thing may run across sentences.

First: for each thing, which of the ten modes it is written in. A fact of the world reported
as it happened is History; an assertion about who a character is is Characterization;
staging of what appears on the page is PageDesign; a design of what the reader is to work
out from the page is WorldInference; evidence laid down for a thematic proposition is
ThematicEvidence; a task, a doubt or a remark about the planning work is NotesToSelf; a
real-world model named as inspiration is Analogies; a plan for how the reader is to
experience the story is NarrativeArchitecture; a constraint drawn from or a recontextualizing
of the established source material is Canon; a real-world group or condition a thing stands
for is Allegories. Then say whether that mode is the one the note's own track type declares.

Second: whether the note says what the reader is to get out of it — an effect, a response, a
thing they are to feel, believe, want, enjoy or take away. For each such goal, say whether
one of the ten modes above is written for that kind of goal, or none of them is. Judge the
goal by what it asks of the reader, not by the track the note sits in.

Judge only what the words hold, never what the note could be made to hold, and never what
the note should have said.

## What to produce

- claims: list of line, one per thing the note says, four parts separated by a bar: mode, one of the ten names, or outside all ten and your own name for it | what it says, in your words | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed | fits, one of yes where that mode is the note's own track type's, no where it is another of the ten, or outside where it is outside all ten
- goals: list of line, one per thing the note says the reader is to get out of it, three parts separated by a bar: goal, what the reader is to feel, believe, want, enjoy or take away, in your words | named by, the one of the ten modes written for that kind of goal, or none where none of them is | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed; empty where the note names none
- whole: line, what this note is doing, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside the note: no line or sentence numbers, and no quotation used
only to say where. Never place a thing in a mode because the note's track sits in it; place
it by what the words do. Never say what the note should have held.
