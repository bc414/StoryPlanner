## What you are given

One place in the archived planning database of a long work of fiction — a scene with the
notes on it and on its links, a subject such as a character or a piece of world lore, or a
chapter — carrying only the notes that a mechanical comparison against the author's
conversations with AI models credits to the author's own writing. Notes that were pasted
whole or nearly whole from a model's reply have been left out, so the item may show only
part of what the place holds. Each note is under its own id; on a scene, the notes on each
link are named by the link. A note marked "(carries a borrowed phrasing)" is the author's
note with one sentence in it that matches a model's reply; which sentence is not shown. You
have nothing else: not the pasted notes, not the other places, not the story's text.

## How to read

A register is a way of writing: the manner the words are in, apart from what they are about
— how formal or clipped they are, whose perspective they take, what kind of sentence they
use, whom they address, what the writing is doing. There is no list of registers to choose
from. Name each one you find in your own terms, by what marks it in the words.

Read each note, then each passage in it, a passage being a stretch written one way; a
sentence may hold more than one and a passage may run across sentences. Say which register
each passage is in. Then say where a note changes register, and what marks the change: a
change of person, of tense, of sentence form, of vocabulary, of address, or of what the
words are for. Say also whether passages in different registers stand apart, with a break a
reader would notice, or run into each other inside one sentence or with no break at all.
Judge only what the words show, and never whose words they are.

## What to produce

- passages: list of line, one per passage, five parts separated by a bar: note, the note's id as the item gives it | register, your own name for the way it is written | what marks it, a few words of the note allowed | about, what the passage is about in a few words | joined, one of apart where it stands on its own, or run-in where it runs into a passage in another register inside one sentence or with no break
- shifts: list of line, one per place inside one note where the register changes, four parts separated by a bar: note, the note's id as the item gives it | from, the register before the change | into, the register after it | what marks the change, a few words of the note allowed; empty where no note changes register
- registers: line, every register this item holds, named as in the passages, separated by semicolons
- whole: line, whether this place's notes are written in one register or several, and how they sit together, in one sentence

## Never

Never name a position inside a note beyond the note's own id: no line or sentence numbers,
and no quotation used only to say where. Never judge whose words a note is in, and never
bring a register from outside the item. Never say how a note should have been written.
